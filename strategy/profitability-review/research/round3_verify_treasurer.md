**Short answer: yes, some companies earn on gold they hold, but leasing is the thin, risky way to do it.**

- Gold in a vault pays nothing. Income only appears once someone **lends the metal** or **charges a fee on it**. (H)
- Wholesale lease rates are normally well under 1% a year. (M) The 2-4% "gold yields" offered to retail customers are mostly a credit spread for lending metal to jewellers. (M)
- The cleanest earners on held gold charge a **custody fee paid in metal** (Goldmoney, GLD). They do not lease. (H)
- The best-known retail leasing business, Monetary Metals, had a borrower default in January 2026. (H in the source note; I could not re-fetch it this pass)
- So leasing "works" as a niche credit business. It is not a safe yield.

### How a gold lease works

1. The owner (a vault holder or platform) agrees a lease with a borrower. The borrower is usually a jeweller or refiner.
2. Title passes. The bar leaves "allocated" status. The owner now holds a **promise of metal back**, not the bar.
3. The borrower pays a fee, usually in gold, monthly or at maturity.
4. The borrower posts protection: insurance, a letter of credit, guarantees.
5. At maturity (often 90-180 days) the borrower returns the same weight, or rolls the lease. (M)

**Why wholesale rates are low.** Lease rate ≈ the USD interest rate minus the gold forward premium. When cash pays about 4%, futures usually price about 3.5% contango, so the lease rate is around 0.5%. (L-M) This is also why Tenbin can pay a "yield": cash plus futures captures the cash rate.

**Worked example (illustrative, Low):**
- 1 kg = 32.15 oz × US$4,200 = **US$135,000**
- *Wholesale lease at 0.5%:* US$675 a year
- *Jeweller lease at 3%:* US$4,050 a year, about 0.96 oz
- Minus insurance and handling (about 1%) and origination (about 1%): **platform keeps about 1% = US$1,350**
- **One default loses US$135,000.** That is about **100 years** of spread.

### Risks that have actually blown up

- **Jeweller default.** AgaBullion (2026): principals arrested and gold seized. Recovery runs through Istanbul courts plus insurance. (M) In India, Winsome Diamonds (about ₹6,800 cr) and Kanishk Gold (₹824 cr) left banks with losses on gold loans. (M)
- **Weak collateral.** A jeweller sells its stock in the normal course of business, so the collateral shrinks just when you need it. (H)
- **Platform failure.** Celsius and BlockFi paid interest on PAXG. Both went bankrupt in 2022. Celsius depositors became unsecured creditors. (M)
- **Price rally hits whoever owes the gold.** GMS and Sovereign Gold Bonds were closed partly because the government's gold liability ballooned. (M)
- **Lease spikes.** Early 2025 rates briefly hit about 4-5%, and the Oct 2025 squeeze was mostly in silver. Spikes last weeks, so they cannot fund a steady yield. (L-M)

### What's realistic for a Dubai startup with allocated customer gold

- **Can it lease?** Not the main reserve. Allocated means the customer owns specific bars. Leasing them needs **separate, explicit, opt-in consent**, and turns the customer into a creditor. (H)
- **If tokenised under VARA:** rule III.C.3.b bars encumbering reserves where that would block redemption. Leasing reserve gold is very hard to fit within it. (H)
- **Licence:** pooling client gold and lending it looks like lending or deposit-taking. That points to **CBUAE**. In the UAE, jeweller metal loans sit with banks, for example CBD's XAU Gold Metal Loan (June 2026; page blocked, not re-checked). (M) Sharia: a fixed return on lent gold carries riba risk. A compliant version needs a Mudaraba or Wakala structure with variable profit. (M)
- **Spread it could keep:** about **0.5-1% on the opt-in leased slice only**, after insurance. On US$10M leased that is about US$50-100k a year. One bad borrower wipes it out. (L)
- **Better options:**
  1. A **custody fee charged in metal** (the Goldmoney model). It grows with the gold price and needs no lending. (H)
  2. A **separate credit book**, where the credit risk is explicit and priced.
  3. If leasing comes later, run it through a **licensed bank partner**, as an opt-in product kept apart from the reserve.
