### 4.13 Comtech Gold (CGO)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Issuer | ComTech FZCO (DAFZA), Dubai Airport Free Zone, licence 05069. Separate ComTech FZCO (DMCC) holds licence DMCC-889799 (cert DMCC197166). #### What it is

Comtech Gold sells one gram of Dubai-vaulted gold per token on XDC. Live since 2022, it runs two products off one reserve: a tradeable token listed on centralised exchanges, and an app-based savings product priced in AED with KYC onboarding and a UAE bank account. The primary domain `comtechgold.com` now redirects to `cgold.ae`, the retail app site. Its public credentials are a December 2022 DMCC partnership registering bars on the Tradeflow warrant platform, and Shariah certification, originally from Amanie Advisors and now from Sābiq Advisory.

This is the closest UAE analogue to what Aurumix intends to build: retail-facing, AED-priced, app-first, aimed at the same Indian expatriate and Gulf mass-market segment. It is also small, with on-chain supply of 39,000 CGO against a $5.04M market capitalisation.

And it has gone quiet. Reserves fell 23% over the last published period, attestations stopped in March 2025, no dated announcements followed in 18 months, the CEO named in the 2022 DMCC release is no longer listed, and the licence dates on the company's own registration page have passed. The app keeps taking retail money.

#### Legal structure and regulatory standing

DAFZA licence 05069 was issued to ComTech FZCO on 21 February 2022 for "gold and other precious metals trading". A separate entity, ComTech FZCO (DMCC), holds DMCC-889799, issued 26 June 2023, for IT consultancy, network consultancy, internet consultancy and software house activity. Both come from Comtech's own page at `cgold.ae/companyregistration`.

Neither licence permits issuing a virtual asset to the public. A precious-metals licence authorises trading bullion; a software-house licence authorises writing software. VARA's remit covers the Emirate of Dubai including all free zones except DIFC, DAFZA and DMCC among them, and issuing a virtual asset to the public is a VARA-regulated activity. Comtech appears on none of the VARA register of 52 licensed virtual asset service providers, the DFSA register or the ADGM FSRA register. Trade Fintech Ltd sits in DIFC as a registered private company with no DFSA financial services licence.

Both licences display as expired on Comtech's own live page: DAFZA expiry 20 February 2024, DMCC expiry 25 June 2024. The page was probably never updated after renewal. What the public record supports is narrower: current good standing cannot be confirmed, and this is what a business accepting retail deposits publishes about itself.

The FAQ then makes an affirmative regulatory claim that does not hold. It states that ComTech Gold "is regulated by Dubai Airport Free Zone Authority (DAFZA) and endorsed by local UAE government body DMCC" and follows "the regulatory guidelines towards the capital reserve, consumer protection, compliance, and anti-money laundering". A free zone authority is a registrar and licensor, not a financial regulator, and it imposes no capital reserve or consumer-protection regime on a bullion trader.

On what a holder owns: clause 3.2 grants undivided ownership of a specific interest in one gram of at least 999 purity gold, and clause 3.6 has the holder irrevocably appoint Comtech as custodian and agent in possession indefinitely. Clause 3.4 asserts the token "is not intended to be a 'security'", an issuer statement no regulator has tested. Clause 15.4 caps issuer liability at fees received. Governing law is English, disputes go to LCIA arbitration seated in DIFC before a single arbitrator, which puts any remedy out of retail reach. Clause 3.5 permits up to 10 kg of tokens to sit unbacked where holidays or logistics prevent same-day purchase, corrected within 24 hours of the next business day. Against a 111 kg reserve, that is a permitted under-backing window of roughly 9% sitting underneath a "100% backed at all times" headline.

#### Custody and reserve verification

Transguard, an Emirates Group company, holds the metal. The terms provide for segregation, and the whitepaper describes 1 kg bars at 999 purity identifiable by bar number. No bar-number list has ever been published. The clause permitting storage "in the UAE or elsewhere", and through affiliates "anywhere in the world", is broader than the marketing suggests.

Comtech publishes gold-stock letters, not audits. Each is on ComTech FZCO letterhead, signed "For ComTech FZCO", with no independent examiner named. The series covers eight periods between 1 May 2022 and 21 March 2025, and the interval stretches from about three weeks at the start to a single 17-month gap at the end. The last letter reports 111 kg, split 19 kg on DMCC Tradeflow and 92 kg with a "vaulting partner", down from 144 kg after 62 kg of withdrawals. That is the first withdrawal activity in the whole series, a 23% net decline, unexplained, with nothing published in the 16 months since.

There is no smart-contract audit and no independent reserve attestation; Comtech's "regularly audited" marketing refers to the gold-stock letters, not to code. Separately, 111 kg of reserve does not reconcile against 39 kg of on-chain supply. The likely explanation is that most of the retail app book is unminted, so customers hold database entries rather than tokens and the vault backs both. If so, the majority of Comtech's customer liability sits off-chain with no on-chain verifiability, and the letters do not split the two figures.

On Shariah, the operative document is a fatwa dated 31 March 2026 from Sābiq Advisory, signed by Dr Mohamed Ali Elgari, Dr Muhammad Amin Ali Qattan and Dr Osama Al Dereai. It supersedes the 6 September 2022 Amanie Advisors fatwa and applies prospectively from the date of appointment. The change of certifier was announced nowhere and is discoverable only by opening the PDF. The fatwa expressly excludes "any distinct financing, leverage, or yield-generation mechanics" from its scope. Clause 14.3 disclaims any warranty of Shariah compliance and puts the determination on the holder; clause 18.1 bars the holder from ever challenging it.

#### Fees, revenue and redemption

The published fee table and the live app disagree, and the difference is the entire revenue model. The website lists transaction, custody and insurance fees as "Nil", with 0.50% for transfers and 0.50% for physical redemption. The live app configuration endpoint returns a buy commission of 1.0125%, a sell commission of 0.9950%, an AED payment charge of 1%, a card and mobile-wallet charge of 2.4%, a USD charge of 0.27% and 5% VAT. Quoted buy and sell rates carry a round-trip spread of roughly 1.8%.

So a retail buyer pays roughly 1% commission, up to 2.4% in payment charges, VAT, and the spread. Comtech funds itself from margin at the point of sale, not from custody. Because nothing is ever deducted in grams, 1 token stays exactly 1 gram and the peg never drifts. The cost is that revenue depends on transaction volume, which is thin for a buy-and-hold product, and Comtech has visibly felt it: the FAQ reserves the right to introduce a storage fee after the first 24 months, clause 11.1.6 lists vault and insurance costs as chargeable, and clause 11.4 permits unilateral fee changes effective on posting.

Redemption is where the binding document and the marketing part company. Clause 8.1 sets the minimum at 1 kilogram, in further one-kilogram denominations, and whitepaper section 3.4 agrees. The FAQ and website advertise 10 grams, thereafter in 1 gram multiples, and whitepaper section 4.7 describes 10 g as available "at a later stage" through redemption partners. The reconciliation: 1 kg is the contractual right to bar delivery, and the 10 g route is a discretionary retail service delivered as minted coins with making charges or through partner jewellers. A holder's enforceable right is 1 kg. At roughly $129 per gram, that is about $129,000, which excludes essentially every retail holder. Delivery outside the UAE reaches the nearest airport only, with the customer handling customs. Orders are irrevocable once placed. Only the holder may redeem, and on death legal heirs may claim with a death certificate and proof of heirship. Clause 16.2 permits the issuer to suspend redemption for technical reasons "or otherwise".

The token pays no yield. Terms Part B, clause 20, expressly outside the Shariah certification, describes a facility under which Comtech may lease pooled reserves to commercial parties in the gold and jewellery business for up to six months in return for a margin. Holders opt in, their tokens lock, and the underlying gold is released. The terms state that the issuer "will not be liable to the Financed Party or the Participating Digital Gold Holder and will simply facilitate the transaction" and that participating holders "will take the risk". Whether the facility has ever been activated is not disclosed.

#### Liquidity and distribution

CGO trades at roughly $129.31 with 24-hour volume of roughly $814,566 as at 28 July 2026. Of that, $814,072 (99.94%) is a single CGO/USDT pair on Bitrue at a 0.42% spread. BitMart turns over $280 at a 3.17% spread; the XSwap V3 pool turns over $213 at 2.21%. Genuine on-chain liquidity is about $200 a day. If Bitrue delists or halts, the market disappears. Note that on its main venue CGO trades at gold parity with a tight spread. Where a gold token has market makers and a working redemption channel, arbitrage closes any gap.

Distribution is app-first and UAE-centric: iOS and Android, AED-denominated, KYC'd, 0.5 g minimum purchase, 800 COMTECH phone line. Two channels reach Aurumix's exact target segment. Kalyan Jewellers is a named "Authorised Partner" for redemption with its store locator linked from the site, serving the Indian expatriate and Gulf market directly. The app configuration also carries a live referral code, `LULU25`, pointing to a Lulu retail group tie-up. Banking runs through ZAND Bank in AED and USD, a genuine achievement for a gold-token business in the UAE.

What Comtech does not have: no savings plan, no systematic investment plan, no recurring contribution product, no agent or commission tier structure, no lock-in. It sells one-off purchases.

#### Relevance to Aurumix

Comtech is the local precedent, and the licensing lesson is negative. It issues a gold token to the public from Dubai on a bullion-trading licence and an IT-consultancy licence, neither of which authorises virtual asset issuance, and it appears on no regulator's register. There is no Dubai gold-token path that routes around VARA, only an absent authorisation nobody has closed. Anyone arguing that Comtech does this on a free-zone trade licence so Aurumix can too should be shown the FAQ passage telling retail customers they are regulated by DAFZA with the highest form of protection. That kind of statement attracts enforcement rather than deflecting it. The ARVA-under-VARA plan remains the defensible route, and the ADGM Accepted Spot Commodity recognition Tether Gold obtained in July 2026 is the live lower-cost alternative worth scoping.

The custody-fee mechanism is the genuinely useful finding. Comtech charges no custody fee and recovers cost through roughly 1% commission plus a roughly 1.8% spread at transaction time. Nothing is ever deducted in grams, so 1 token = 1 gram holds exactly and the peg never drifts. That validates Aurumix's instinct to avoid gram-denominated fees. The catch is severe for Aurumix specifically: transaction-margin funding only works if people transact, and a monthly SIP designed to build a holding collects that margin once on the way in, then carries storage cost indefinitely. Comtech reserving a storage fee after 24 months is the tell that this does not close on its own.

From the rest:

- **Redemption honesty is available cheaply.** Comtech advertises 10 g redemption while its binding minimum is 1 kg, roughly $129,000, or about 1,700 times a $75-per-month saver's position. Aurumix's cash-only exit is the more honest posture and should be framed explicitly against this comparison.
- **Copy the succession handling.** Heir claims with a death certificate and proof of heirship map directly onto the Digital Will.
- **The dividend has a Shariah constraint.** The Sābiq fatwa carves financing and yield generation out of its scope. Gold certification does not cover a yield mechanism, and the ICS Dividend will need its own ruling for Islamic-market credibility.
- **Copy the leasing disclosure, not the risk allocation.** Part B is opt-in, locks the tokens while lent and discloses per-deal terms. It also dumps the entire counterparty default loss on the holder while the issuer "will simply facilitate". Publish a loss waterfall instead.
- **A plain ERC-20 destroys a rights bundle.** Comtech can afford a non-permissioned XRC-20 because its token carries no rights beyond one gram. Aurumix's ICS standing, dividend entitlement, credit eligibility and buyback rights would all die on the first anonymous transfer.
- **Do not model a premium.** CGO trades at parity at a 0.42% spread on its only real venue. A durable 3% to 8% premium signals illiquidity or capital controls, not healthy tokenised gold.

On proof of reserve, the bar Comtech sets is low: self-signed letters, no bar list despite the whitepaper promising bar-number identifiability, a cadence stretching to 17 months, then silence after a 23% reserve decline. Aurumix can beat that with a genuine independent attestation on a fixed cadence, a real bar list, and clear separation of "smart-contract audit" from "reserve attestation". Comtech also proves attestation practice decays, so whatever cadence Aurumix commits to has to survive years.

On wind-down, Comtech's FAQ asserts that because gold movements are endorsed by DMCC, "in any unlikely adverse event happening to Company, the gold will not be affected". A Tradeflow warrant records title to bullion. It creates no bankruptcy-remote structure, and clause 15.4 caps issuer liability at fees received. There is no trustee, no security trustee, no independent custodian holding for holders. A named trustee, a defined waterfall and a pre-agreed liquidation agent remain cheap differentiation, and Comtech's answer is the ideal foil to quote against. Finally, Aurumix will face the same settlement lag between customer payment and bullion purchase that clause 3.5 accommodates. Decide now whether to disclose a float, size it and cap it, rather than burying permission for it in a clause.

### 4.14 ORO / Oro Finance (GOLD)

#### What it is

ORO is a tokenised gold protocol on Solana, one GOLD to one troy ounce of certified physical gold. Its pitch against PAXG and XAUT is that the gold is not merely parked: holders stake GOLD into an institutional gold leasing programme and earn yield paid in more GOLD, so the position compounds in metal rather than fiat.

The operating company is a Singapore holding company, the product is marketed from Dubai, and the founder's contact details are UAE-based. ORO raised a $1.5M pre-seed in March 2025 led by 468 Capital, with Fasset participating alongside angels from Phantom, Jupiter and Helium. Usman Saleem is founder and CEO. Juan Marchetto, co-founder and CTO, built the Solana protocol and has since left for another company; current materials describe the company as founded by Saleem alone.

It is small and unusually retail: 608 ounces, roughly $2.45M, across about 9,700 to 9,800 holders. That averages 0.0625 oz, or roughly $252 each. On that shape it is the closest structural analogue to what Aurumix intends to build.

The gap between plan and outcome is worth measuring. The December 2024 launch release announced ORO as the first application built on Fasset's Ethereum Layer 2 network, "Own", targeting $1 billion of tokenised gold in the first year. ORO shipped on Solana instead, with no bridge and no deployment on Own or any EVM Layer 2, and never announced that the Own deployment had been dropped. Actual AUM 19 months later is roughly 0.245% of the target.

#### Legal structure and regulatory standing

The documentation states that "legal title is held by a foundation, making the structure bankruptcy remote", names it the "ORO Foundation", describes holders as having "beneficial ownership of all underlying physical assets" through a trust structure, and calls GOLD "a digital bearer instrument for ownership of real gold".

The ORO Foundation is not identifiable in any register. Searches of the Cayman Islands General Registry, the Panama Public Registry, ADGM, Labuan, the Liechtenstein Stiftungsregister and Singapore ACRA return no match. No jurisdiction for it is stated anywhere in ORO's own documentation. No foundation charter, trust deed or declaration of trust is published. The word "foundation" appears in the docs and in one Solana ecosystem article, nowhere else. So the chain of title runs holder, to beneficial interest, to a trust, to a foundation that cannot be shown to exist. Bankruptcy remoteness follows from a specific entity in a specific jurisdiction with specific constitutional documents. None of the three is disclosed.

The Singapore entity does not hold itself out as the issuer. The terms attribute issuance to unnamed "independent Third-Party Service Providers", and Oro Labs PTE Ltd disclaims the issuing role. RWA aggregators list "Gold Issuance Inc.", which matches nothing in any register searched. The identity of the legally liable issuer is not established from public sources.

ORO holds no licence anywhere. Secondary write-ups circulate a claim that its token is regulated by the Labuan FSA. No entity named ORO, Oro Finance, Oro Labs or Orogold appears on any Labuan FSA licence category, nor on the Labuan warning list. ORO is also reported to have signed a Memorandum of Understanding at ADGM on tokenised gold adoption in the UAE. An MoU is not a licence and confers no authorisation, and no ADGM press release or register entry confirming it could be located.

The KYC language does not match the token. The docs state that "only KYC/KYB whitelisted wallets can mint, convert, and redeem" and that "all redemption options are available only to KYC/KYB whitelisted wallets". On-chain, the mint's freeze authority is null. There is no transfer restriction, no permissioning, no whitelist enforceable at the token layer. GOLD is a plain SPL token anyone can buy on Jupiter or Meteora with no KYC, and secondary coverage advertises exactly that. The gate sits at the mint and redeem boundary with the issuer, not on the asset. That is a coherent design. The phrasing invites the reader to believe otherwise.

#### Custody and reserve verification

Brinks is named as custodian, framed as "Brinks handles the physical vaulting, not Oro". No custody agreement is published and the vault city is not disclosed. Allocation and segregation are implied by the 1:1 backing claim and the trust framing but never stated as contractual terms. No bar list exists: no serial numbers, no weights, no refiner list. No proof-of-reserve oracle, no Chainlink feed, no live reserve dashboard.

The audit and attestation blur is severe. The transparency page is headed "Independently Verified Reserves", and the only two documents downloadable from it are smart-contract security audits. A smart-contract audit confirms the Solana program behaves as coded. It says nothing about whether 608 ounces sit in a vault. A reader who skims "audited" draws the wrong conclusion.

Attestation frequency is inconsistent across ORO's own surfaces. The front page and transparency page say reserves are verified quarterly by RSM. The FAQ refers to monthly audit reports from third-party vault and assurance providers. A Solana ecosystem article says monthly audits from RSM. Three frequencies from one issuer, and no published document to settle it.

#### Fees, revenue and redemption

Minting swaps USDC for GOLD at 1:1 minus 0.50%, rising to 1.00% outside market hours, and requires government ID plus liveness for individuals or business registration and signatory verification for entities. No minimum purchase is stated. On-chain, the mint authority is a program-derived address under program `iNtiXEFgDNrc6FUt4cFALDe3D8RF3sVnNuKSHwxZRop`, so minting is program-controlled rather than sitting on a bare wallet. The program's upgrade authority is not disclosed, and it is the most important unverified control over the token. The mint was created 27 April 2025, first liquidity pool 21 May 2025.

Redemption to USDC costs 0.50% (1.00% off-hours) plus a 1.5% settlement charge, takes up to 48 hours, and locks the price at request against live spot. Physical redemption starts at 1 g increments, takes 3 to 7 business days, and carries delivery, insurance and a market premium quoted before confirmation but never published as a rate. Both routes are gated to KYC and KYB whitelisted wallets, and physical pickup is UAE only. Redemption "may be unavailable in restricted jurisdictions", which are not listed. A holder in India who bought GOLD without KYC on Jupiter has no assured physical exit.

ORO charges no ongoing custody or management fee. Staking is free, claiming yield is free, unstaking after the lockup is free. Cost is recovered from the leasing spread and the mint and redeem fees, which preserves an exact 1 token = 1 ounce peg, the property Aurumix needs. The arithmetic does not close at this size. Allocated vaulted custody and insurance on $2.45M run roughly 0.10% to 0.50% a year, so $2,500 to $12,000. Against that, ORO must pay stakers 3% to 5%, which requires the leasing programme to earn more than that gross, and institutional gold lease rates have historically sat well below those levels in normal conditions. Even a full 1% round trip on the entire supply turning over once a year is about $25,000. The $1.5M pre-seed is funding the operation, not the fee model.

The Monetary Metals relationship is real, both parties confirm it, and it appears only in marketing. No lease agreement, master lease or term sheet naming ORO is published. No subordination, security interest, collateral or priority provision naming GOLD holders exists in any public document. No loss waterfall allocating a lessee default between the token holder, ORO and Monetary Metals has been published. No Monetary Metals legal document names ORO at all.

ORO's documentation asserts the outcome without the instrument: "in case of lessee insolvency, leased gold is not part of their estate", the gold "legally remains your property even while leased", and "all gold under leases is insured against theft and damage". Monetary Metals has published a proper tokenised lease agreement, just not with ORO. Its Tokenized Yield Partnership Agreement with StreamEx, filed with the SEC, requires the lessee to maintain a jeweller's block policy at 110% or more of gold value, with Monetary Metals as loss payee and additional insured, from an A-rated or better insurer. Monetary Metals separately describes requiring corporate guarantees, UCC filings and sometimes personal guarantees. The disclosure standard exists and is achievable. ORO has not met it.

The risk is not theoretical. On 26 January 2026 Monetary Metals disclosed a default at AgaBullion Kiymetli Madenler A.Ş. Turkish authorities arrested the principals of AgaBullion and the affiliated Aleks Metal Refinery, and some leased gold was seized. Monetary Metals stated that legal title remains with lessors, that it is working with Istanbul counsel and the US Consulate, and that it "carries supplemental insurance on leased metals and will file claims when and if appropriate". That is a multi-year, cross-border, uncertain recovery. Note the ordering too: the loss payee on the lessee's insurance is Monetary Metals, not the token holder, so proceeds reach the end holder only through whatever contract exists between them, which for ORO is unpublished.

#### Liquidity and distribution

GOLD trades at roughly $4,032.71 against about $812,000 of pooled liquidity across all decentralised exchanges. The main pool is Meteora GOLD/USDC at roughly $736,000 of liquidity and $54,800 of 24-hour volume, with smaller pools against XAUt0 (roughly $70,500 on Meteora), GLDx (roughly $3,200) and XAUt0 on Orca (roughly $1,200). Total 24-hour volume is about $56,800. Top-holder concentration is roughly 61.85%, and Jupiter assigns the token a low organic score.

That is a 33% liquidity-to-AUM ratio, roughly sixteen times healthier than PGOLD's, and it still produces no premium. GOLD prints $4,032 to $4,041 across pools while XAUt0 prints around $4,079 in the paired pool, so ORO trades at a slight discount to another tokenised gold token rather than a premium to spot. Volume is one-directional: roughly $55,200 of sells against $1,600 of buys in 24 hours, about 34 to 1, with pooled liquidity down 14.3% on the day.

Distribution runs through the app at `app.oro.finance` plus permissionless access on Jupiter, Meteora, Orca and Raydium. The December 2024 launch material named the UAE, Indonesia, Turkey, Malaysia, the EU, Pakistan and India as target markets, with explicit focus on Muslim-majority markets across Asia and the Middle East. That overlaps Aurumix's India, UAE and non-resident Indian target almost exactly. Retail growth comes from a "Nuggets" points programme and a widely anticipated airdrop, not from a savings product: no monthly contribution plan, no systematic investment feature, no referral commission structure, no agent network. Points programmes produce holders who leave when the airdrop lands, which is a plausible reading of that 34-to-1 sell-to-buy ratio.

#### Relevance to Aurumix

ORO is the most instructive protocol here, because it is the only one attempting the same three things at once: retail-scale distribution, a yield on top of gold, and no custody fee charged to the holder. On the question Aurumix's dividend design hinges on, it is a cautionary tale.

Aurumix's central hope is that real external asset yield from gold leasing is the compliant alternative to a fee-recycled dividend. ORO is the live test, and the result is that the legal plumbing does not exist. The leasing relationship is real and mutually confirmed, and it appears only in marketing. Nothing published establishes how GOLD holders rank if a lease counterparty defaults. If Aurumix funds its dividend from gold leasing to avoid the securities-classification risk of a fee-funded dividend, it inherits lessee credit risk on the very gold that backs the token. Leased gold is gold that is not in the vault. Aurumix should decide explicitly, in writing before launch, whether a defaulting lessee's loss lands on all holders pro rata and breaks the peg, on only the stakers who opted in, or on the issuer's balance sheet. Publishing that waterfall would be genuine differentiation, and nobody here has done it.

On the custody fee, ORO validates the mechanism and not the arithmetic. It charges holders nothing ongoing, preserving an exact 1 token = 1 ounce peg, and funds custody from the leasing spread plus mint and redeem fees. Aurumix should copy the structure and be honest internally that it needs scale, or a genuinely profitable adjacent revenue line such as the credit facility and Gold Card, to fund it.

The rest, in short:

- **ORO is the counterfactual on token standard.** A plain SPL token with freeze authority null earns a 33% liquidity-to-AUM ratio and real decentralised exchange quotability. It pays for that by making every KYC claim in its documentation unenforceable on the asset. Aurumix cannot make this trade, because ICS standing, dividend entitlement, credit eligibility and buyback rights all die on an anonymous transfer. Expect materially thinner secondary liquidity, and plan the buyback as the primary exit rather than hoping a market appears.
- **Physical redemption can be geographically hollow.** ORO offers physical from 1 g, which sounds decisive against a cash-only buyback, but pickup is UAE-only, whitelisting is required, and delivery premiums stay undisclosed until quoted. For holders in Indonesia, Turkey, Malaysia, Pakistan and India the option is largely notional. Aurumix's cash-buyback-only position is more honest, provided Aurumix never claims physical redeemability. The reputational risk sits in the mismatch, not the absence.
- **The premium thesis fails from the liquid end too.** ORO refutes it with deep liquidity as PGOLD refutes it with none. Stop treating a 3% to 8% exchange premium as a modelling input. No tokenised gold token here sustains one, and structurally none can while the issuer holds a live mint authority.
- **ORO is the proof-of-reserve anti-pattern to name.** "Independently Verified Reserves" as a headline, two smart-contract audits as the only downloadable evidence, three mutually inconsistent attestation frequencies, no bar list, no proof-of-reserve feed, no attestation document. One real attestation with a bar list on a stated cadence would out-disclose a venture-backed competitor immediately.
- **Distribution remains uncontested.** ORO has no savings plan, no recurring purchase and no agent network. Aurumix's SIP plus three-tier agent model is unmatched across this entire set.

On wind-down, ORO is weaker than silence. It gestures at a bankruptcy-remote foundation that cannot be found in any register in any jurisdiction, inviting reliance on a protection nobody can verify exists. Combine that with the departure of the CTO who built the protocol and an undisclosed program upgrade authority, and there is key-person and key-control risk with no published contingency. Committing ex ante to a named entity, a named jurisdiction, a published charter and a wind-down waterfall remains cheap and unoccupied ground. One caution about the genre: ORO's launch release named a chain it did not ship on and a first-year AUM target it missed by more than 400 times. Treat tokenised gold launch announcements as marketing until the contract address confirms otherwise.

### 4.15 WisdomTree Gold Token (WTGOLD)

#### What it is

WTGOLD is a token on Stellar and Ethereum, each unit representing one troy ounce of LBMA Good Delivery gold held at HSBC Bank plc in London. It is sold to US retail consumers through the WisdomTree Prime mobile app alongside WT Dollar and hosted crypto. It launched on 22 September 2022 and remains purchasable as of July 2026.

The distinguishing feature is legal rather than technical, and Aurumix should understand it. WTGOLD is not a tokenised fund share. It is an electronic document of title under Articles 1, 2 and 7 of the New York Uniform Commercial Code, issued by a bailee. Not a fund, not a security on the issuer's own analysis, not registered with the SEC, and it has no transfer agent. Ownership is recorded on the blockchain exclusively. In legal substance, a digital warehouse receipt.

WisdomTree does run a separate tokenised business on the transfer-agent model: WisdomTree Digital Funds (WTGXX and around fourteen siblings) are Investment Company Act of 1940 registered mutual funds whose shares are recorded by a registered transfer agent with a blockchain mirror. None is a gold fund. A full-text search of the entire 374,000-character WisdomTree Digital Trust prospectus returns zero occurrences of the word "gold". The two businesses are structurally unrelated.

WTGOLD is also a rounding error inside its parent. WisdomTree, Inc. manages over $150bn, and its tokenised assets under management reached roughly $770M by the second quarter of 2026, a 25-fold increase since 2024. WTGOLD sits at roughly $2M and has received no product-specific development in 18 months.

#### Legal structure and regulatory standing

The operative contract is the WisdomTree Prime User Agreement, last updated 31 March 2026, and it says what the instrument is in terms. Section 9 has the user agree that WT Gold and other so-designated WT Digital Assets "represent an 'electronic document of title' to LBMA Gold... for purposes of Articles 1, 2 and 7 of the New York Uniform Commercial Code". Section 1(m) defines WT Gold as "a digital token recorded on a blockchain... representing an electronic document of title to and ownership of one-troy ounce of physical gold" meeting LBMA good delivery requirements. The phrase "and ownership of" is stronger than most tokens here. The holder is asserted to own the gold, not merely to hold a claim.

Section 9(j) names the bailment: "WT TrustCo acts as the bailee which issued the electronic document of title." Section 9(d) makes it a bearer instrument: "User is the bearer and holder of the electronic documents of title represented by the WT Digital Assets held in User's Wallet." The bailee never takes title, so the goods are not the bailee's property and in principle are not available to the bailee's general creditors.

The contract draws the line deliberately. Section 10 is titled "Security Entitlements" and applies UCC Article 8 to hosted crypto such as BTC and ETH, treating them as financial assets credited to a securities account with WisdomTree Digital as securities intermediary. WT Gold is expressly not in section 10. It sits in section 9 under Article 7. WisdomTree's lawyers separated an Article 8 intermediated security entitlement from an Article 7 bearer document of title and put gold on the Article 7 side.

Section 9(j) also contains a Substitute Bailee clause: WT TrustCo may transfer its obligations and substitute an affiliate as bailee, covenanting only that the substitute will be duly authorised and licensed. The user consents in advance, notice goes to the WisdomTreePrime.com website, and the user then looks "solely to the Substitute Bailee" for transfer or delivery of the gold. That is a continuity mechanism, not a liquidation plan, and it is the nearest thing to a wind-down provision in the document.

On perimeter, WisdomTree occupies the money transmission and virtual currency space, not the securities space. NYDFS granted the trust company charter on 22 March 2024, permitting digital asset custody and stablecoin issuance and allowing Prime to launch in New York; the charter number is not disclosed in any source located. Section 23(j) asserts that WisdomTree maintains state money transmission licences and points to a State License Disclosure page, which returned HTTP 403, so the state list and NMLS numbers are not available.

There is no SEC registration of WTGOLD and no exemption claimed. The token document states that "Gold Tokens and this document have not been registered pursuant the securities or commodity laws of any country". An EDGAR full-text search for "WisdomTree Gold Token" returns zero hits across all form types and all dates, verified directly against `efts.sec.gov`; "Gold Digital Fund" also returns zero. No CFTC registration was found. WisdomTree Securities, Inc. is an SEC-registered broker-dealer, but section 8 confines its involvement to WT Digital Fund Share transactions, not WT Gold. The compliance logic is coherent: WisdomTree obtained no securities exemption because on its own analysis it needs none. A document of title to a physical commodity is not a security.

#### Custody and reserve verification

HSBC Bank plc holds the gold in London. WisdomTree asserts that "HSBC has contractually committed to maintain a continuous allocation (i.e., segregation) process whereby each gold bar associated with a Gold Token is segregated within the gold custodian's vaults with a serial number in accordance with LBMA's good delivery standards". The HSBC agreement is not published, so allocation is an issuer assertion rather than a verified term.

Nothing else is published. No bar list. No proof-of-reserve oracle or equivalent feed. No periodic independent attestation of the gold: no named auditor, no frequency, no report. The documentation carries a page headed "Independent Audits", and no attestation document could be retrieved from it. There is also no smart-contract audit. So WTGOLD has neither a reserve attestation nor a smart-contract audit. The materiality of that gap is lower here than elsewhere for reasons that do not transfer: the parent is an SEC-reporting NYSE-listed company whose consolidated financial statements are audited annually and whose 10-K describes the Prime business, and the custodian is HSBC rather than an unnamed vault operator. The exposure is that a holder has no independent, contemporaneous, per-token way to verify the gold. That is a transparency failure, not a solvency signal.

The on-chain namespace makes it worse. WisdomTree publishes no contract or issuer address in its token document, its documentation site or the User Agreement, and serves no `stellar.toml` at `wisdomtreeprime.com`, `wisdomtree.com` or `www.wisdomtree.com`. Without a `stellar.toml` there is no cryptographic way for a holder to identify which Stellar issuer account is genuinely WisdomTree's.

Every Stellar asset currently named "WTGOLD" or "WisdomTree" that could be located is a spoof, verified against Horizon and stellar.expert on 28 July 2026. The larger of two assets coded `WTGOLD`, issuer `GBSCY7XCPOJEUGRQU77SIXYNXCOUKF33EZ7L27W2XXZXSS6N667N7E37`, carries home domain `wisdomtree.xlmhq.org`, a supply of 1,511,450,381 units, four trustlines, four payments and all authorization flags set to false. A genuine regulated bailee issuing a permissioned document of title would not leave `auth_required`, `auth_revocable` and clawback disabled. A broader search returns a cluster of spoofed assets on domains including `wisdomtree.com.co`, `wisdomtree.bond`, `cadence.wisdomtree-stellar.com`, `micron.wisdomtree-stellar.com`, `globaltoken-stellar.com` and `stocks.kraken.com.bz`, impersonating real WisdomTree ETF tickers such as DXJ, DFJ, WTMF, AIVL, NTSE and QSIG with supplies running to 1.8 quadrillion units.

For an instrument whose legal theory rests on section 9(e), under which the blockchain "will be the exclusive means of delivering and determining who has control", that is a serious gap. The issuer has made the ledger legally dispositive while publishing nothing that lets a holder identify the correct ledger entry.

#### Fees, revenue and redemption

WisdomTree charges 0% to subscribe or mint and 0% ongoing for custody, storage or management. On-chain transfers out pass through gas plus a WisdomTree fee "designed to cover" network costs, disclosed pre-trade under section 7(d). Fee changes take 48 hours' notice by email under section 6, and WisdomTree may update its Digital Assets Schedule at its discretion, prospectively. Section 23 reserves the right to deduct an administrative fee on unclaimed property escheatment.

The in-app buy and sell spread is not published anywhere. That is the single largest disclosure gap in the product, because it is almost certainly the primary revenue line. Two other things fund the zero fee. Users must convert USD into WT Dollars to buy gold, and WT TrustCo holds the WT Dollar reserve in Treasury bills, reverse repo, government money market funds and bank deposits under section 11(b), so gold purchases generate dollar-token float that earns real money at 2026 rates. Beyond that, a $150bn listed parent can run a $2M gold product at a loss indefinitely as a customer-acquisition and optionality play.

Redemption terms are quotable verbatim from the operative contract, which is rare here. Section 5(h)(i) requires the holder to hold a minimum of one full ounce, contact customer support between 9am and 9pm EST, submit a completed WT Gold Retrieval Form to `DAOps@wisdomtree.com`, pass identity and delivery-address verification, accept the terms of the independent Metal Agent facilitating delivery, and agree to pay all applicable fees, taxes and shipping. Residual value below a whole ounce returns as WT Dollars at the LBMA price at the time of retrieval, not as metal. Section 5(g) cancels surrendered tokens immediately, and the user "will not be entitled to return of any WT Digital Assets so transferred for exchange and cancellation".

Section 5(h)(ii) sets the fee: "In connection with retrieving LBMA Gold by surrendering WT Gold, User agrees to pay WisdomTree Digital a fee, as required by the Metal Agent, of the greater of: (i) the price of one-troy ounce of LBMA Gold; or (ii) 2% of the total value of WT Gold to be surrendered."

The floor is not 2%. The floor is the entire price of one ounce of gold. The 2% only binds once the redemption exceeds 50 ounces, because 2% of 50 oz equals 1 oz.

| Redemption size | Fee | Effective cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 oz (the minimum) | 1 oz | 100% |
| 2 oz | 1 oz | 50% |
| 5 oz | 1 oz | 20% |
| 10 oz | 1 oz | 10% |
| 25 oz | 1 oz | 4% |
| 50 oz | 1 oz | 2% (crossover) |
| 100 oz and above | 2% | 2% |

A retail holder redeeming the one-ounce minimum surrenders one ounce, pays one ounce, and receives nothing. Physical redemption carries no "qualified holder" gate, which is a genuine plus, but it is economically unavailable below roughly 10 to 25 ounces, or $24,000 to $60,000. Universal eligibility with prohibitive economics does the same work as a qualified-holder restriction and reads better in marketing.

#### Liquidity and distribution

WTGOLD holds roughly $2.0M to $2.5M across about 660 holder addresses. Third-party data reports around 483 tokens, with a total asset value of roughly $1.97M in one snapshot and a net asset value of roughly $2.52M in another. The inconsistency is unresolved and the issuer publishes nothing.

On-chain liquidity and daily volume are zero, and not because the market is thin. There is no market. WTGOLD has no decentralised exchange pool, no centralised exchange listing and no secondary venue. It is buyable and sellable only inside the WisdomTree Prime app, against WisdomTree as principal, at a spread WisdomTree does not publish. Price is administered, not discovered, so the premium question never arises.

Distribution is a single proprietary closed channel: the Prime mobile app on iOS and Android, US retail only, with state availability gated by money transmitter licensing. No exchange listings, no broker distribution, no business-to-business API for gold. No savings plan, no recurring purchase feature, no referral scheme, no affiliate programme, no agent network. As a US-regulated entity, a multi-tier commission structure of the kind Aurumix plans would raise broker-dealer registration questions WisdomTree has clearly avoided. The stated differentiator is payments: WisdomTree's own token document is subtitled "DRIVING TOWARD GOLD YOU CAN SPEND", and coverage of the NYDFS charter referenced a Visa card letting customers pay for coffee with digital gold tokens. The pitch is spendability, not yield and not savings discipline.

The timeline shape is worth noting. Every dated development since 2024 concerns the funds, the transfer agent, the stablecoin or the platform: a 485BXT filing on 24 July 2026, SEC exemptive relief on 23 February 2026 under Investment Company Act Release No. 35968, the December 2025 launch of the WisdomTree Equity Premium Income Digital Fund, and a June 2025 industry award for the blockchain-enabled transfer agent. Not one WTGOLD-specific announcement in 18 months, against a fund business that grew 25-fold. No enforcement actions, incidents, outages or breaches were found across WisdomTree's digital asset business, a clean record and unusual in this set.

#### Relevance to Aurumix

WTGOLD is the flagship precedent for the non-security gold core, and that is worth more to Aurumix than any offshore comparator here.

A NYSE-listed, SEC-reporting, NYDFS-chartered US asset manager with over $150bn under management, operating in the most litigious securities jurisdiction on earth, structured its retail gold token as a non-security document of title to a physical commodity, did not register it with the SEC and claimed no exemption. WisdomTree has a public share price, an audit committee and a legal budget that make an aggressive classification position unaffordable. It reached the same conclusion Aurumix has.

Note precisely what is validated: the bare gold core, and nothing else. WisdomTree owns a registered transfer agent, a broker-dealer and a fund adviser. It holds allocated gold at HSBC. It has every capability required to run a lending programme. It pays zero yield, offers no dividend, provides no credit facility, operates no tiered allocation system and runs no agent network. It could build any of those in a quarter. It chose the clean commodity perimeter and stayed inside it. That is evidence for the gold core and evidence against the hybrid, and it says the classification risk lives in the superstructure Aurumix wants to layer on top.

The fund-wrapper alternative should be retired as a hypothesis. The WisdomTree Digital Trust prospectus states that the transfer agent "maintains the official record of share ownership", that a blockchain transfer "can act as an information source for the Transfer Agent", and that "the Transfer Agent's book-entry records constitute the official record of share ownership". Read the direction of authority: the token is a mirror, not the asset, and if the two disagree the book wins. That solves the securities-classification problem completely, and the price is the entire premise of a token. Possession stops being ownership, transfers must go to wallets known to the transfer agent, every holder must be a KYC'd shareholder of record, and the issuer inherits the full 1940 Act cost base including a board, independent trustees, daily NAV, a prospectus, an annual audit, a chief compliance officer and exemptive applications that took WisdomTree 9.5 months from filing to order. More decisively, the 1940 Act route is not available for a gold product at all. A vehicle holding physical gold is not an investment company under the 1940 Act, which is why US physical gold ETFs such as GLD are 1933 Act grantor trusts with no 1940 Act registration and no 1940 Act protections. WisdomTree did not apply the fund wrapper to gold because the wrapper does not fit gold.

What Aurumix should take:

- **Take the instrument-separation lesson.** WisdomTree runs a non-security gold document of title and registered fund shares side by side in one app, with distinct legal wrappers, distinct regulators, distinct record-keeping and distinct distribution rails, and the wrappers never blend. Kinesis reaches the same answer from a different starting point. Unify the app, sever the wrappers. If Aurumix wants a savings core plus a yield instrument, put them in different legal objects under different licences and unify only in the user experience.
- **A trust-company charter, not a securities licence, is the natural home for a gold custody token.** WisdomTree's perimeter is custody and value transfer. The same logic supports the VARA and ARVA framing for the Aurumix core.
- **Price in the cost of permissioning.** Stellar's built-in authorization flags give WisdomTree the functional equivalent of ERC-3643 permissioning for free, cheaper than a specialised standard. What it accepted in exchange is zero external liquidity, because a permissioned token with no venue is a closed loop. That is the honest price of the permissioned route.
- **Use the redemption maths as a disclosure warning, not a feature to copy.** A one-ounce minimum that costs one ounce is redemption in name only. Aurumix's explicit no-physical-redemption position is a better disclosure posture than a redemption right customers cannot rationally exercise, and the comparison is worth making directly in client materials.
- **Borrow the Substitute Bailee idea, fix its defects.** Naming a successor mechanism ex ante is the closest thing to a documented continuity plan anywhere here. The defects: the substitute must be an affiliate, no holder consent is required, notice is by website posting, and there is no external trustee and no independent insolvency protection.
- **Do not conclude that zero custody fee is free.** Eight protocols now show zero ongoing custody fee. For PAXG and XAUT it is funded by billions in float. For WisdomTree it is funded by an undisclosed spread, dollar-token float and a listed parent's strategic budget. Aurumix has none of those. Recording "the norm is zero" without recording the balance sheet behind it would be the wrong lesson.

Two closing notes. On proof of reserve, both halves fail here: no bar list, no proof-of-reserve feed, no reserve attestation and no smart-contract audit, mitigated but not cured by HSBC as custodian and an audited SEC-reporting parent. Aurumix can out-disclose a NYSE-listed competitor cheaply. On product risk, note the abandonment signature. A zero-revenue gold product inside a fast-growing fund business has received no development in 18 months. WisdomTree has an unrelated strategic reason to keep it alive, but the revenue logic is the same one that stranded PMGT. Aurumix's gold core must generate revenue for someone internally, or it decays the same way.

#### What it is

tGLD is a synthetic, yield-bearing gold exposure token aimed at institutions and decentralised finance collateral markets, not retail savers. Tenbin Labs is a New York tokenisation protocol that raised a $7M seed led by Galaxy Ventures, announced 27 January 2026, with Wintermute Ventures, FalconX, GSR, Nascent, Variant, Archetype and Bankless Ventures participating. Yuki Yuminaga is co-founder and CEO.

The core design choice rejects the custody-wrapper model outright. Rather than buying bullion and issuing a receipt, Tenbin holds USDC and takes long CME gold futures. The consequences: mint and redemption in roughly 30 seconds (about three Ethereum blocks), zero fees under normal conditions, and a yield from futures basis and carry that a physically-backed token structurally cannot produce. Yuminaga's framing is that tokenisation "only works if onchain assets become more useful than their off-chain equivalents". There is a sibling foreign exchange line on the same architecture: tBRL for the Brazilian real and tMXN for the Mexican peso, each with a staked variant.

One naming point for anyone re-running this research. Tenbin tGLD is not Aurus tGOLD. They are unrelated: different tickers (tGLD versus TXAU), different registries, different countries (BVI versus the United Kingdom), different units (one troy ounce versus one gram), different backing (USDC and futures versus physical gold at LBMA refineries), different contracts, an order-of-magnitude price difference and different redemption terms. Anchor on the contract address, not the symbol, since some aggregators render the token as uppercase "TGLD".

The protocol is small but not dormant: deployed and verifiable on-chain, with a public GitHub repository, a live audit trail, a dated May 2026 infrastructure migration and a dated June 2026 institutional integration. The opacity is real and narrow, and it sits almost entirely in the legal documentation layer rather than the technical one.

#### Legal structure and regulatory standing

The holder owns a creditor claim, not gold. tGLD is described as a debt note of Tenbin AssetCo (BVI) SPC representing the USD value of one troy ounce of gold. The holder is a creditor of a BVI segregated portfolio company with no title to metal, because no metal exists in the structure. The structure is reportedly built on UCC Article 12 controllable electronic records, the US framework for negotiable digital assets, a more modern wrapper than most gold tokens use.

The segregated portfolio company point matters and is under-documented. A BVI SPC ring-fences the assets and liabilities of each segregated portfolio from every other portfolio and from the company's general assets. In principle tGLD noteholders have recourse to the gold portfolio's assets and are insulated from losses in the tBRL or tMXN portfolios. That is a genuine structural protection where properly executed, and better than the single-balance-sheet exposure most token issuers offer.

None of it is verifiable from public sources. No Tenbin terms of issue, token terms or offering document setting out the nature of the claim, the ranking of creditors on insolvency, or the operation of the segregation was found. The segregation effect described above is an inference from general BVI law, not a Tenbin disclosure. For a product whose entire investor-protection story rests on portfolio segregation, the absence of a published constitutional document is the largest gap in the profile. Three BVI entities appear on the register alongside TenB International Ltd, and the division of function between them is not disclosed. Which entity employs staff, which holds intellectual property and which contracts with the prime broker all bear on what a noteholder can reach in a failure.

No licence or registration was found in any register, and third-party data classifies the asset as non-regulated. That is not necessarily evasive: a BVI SPC issuing notes to KYC-approved institutional counterparties is a well-trodden structure that can sit outside licensing perimeters by design. It does mean the compliance perimeter is contractual rather than supervisory, enforced through the KYC gate and the restriction registry rather than through a regulator. Compliance runs in code: mint and redemption are KYC-gated through a `Controller` contract and a backend signer holding the on-chain minter role, and a `RestrictedRegistry` contract lists accounts that cannot interact with the controller, checked against both payer and recipient on every order.

Which jurisdictions are excluded, and whether US persons may hold or redeem, is not disclosed. For a New York-headquartered team issuing a futures-hedged instrument, whether the note is offered to US persons is a first-order question with commodity-pool and securities implications, and there is no public answer.

#### Custody and reserve verification

The standard custody table barely applies, which is itself the finding. There is no custodian of metal, no vault, no city, no allocation and no bar list, because there is no gold. Hidden Road and Ripple Prime are reported as prime brokers, with StoneX also mentioned. Collateral is USDC, held on-chain by a `CollateralManager` and posted at the prime broker as CME futures margin.

Tenbin has an unusually strong smart-contract audit record, two rounds across six or more firms including top-tier names, and no reserve attestation at all. A reader skimming "four audits" concludes the reserves are verified. They are not. What partially compensates is that USDC collateral sits on-chain and is directly observable in a way vaulted bullion never is. The futures margin leg sits at a prime broker and is not publicly observable, and that leg is what actually delivers the gold exposure. So the collateral is half-transparent by construction and half-opaque, with no attestation covering the opaque half. The Chainlink feed is a blended price oracle, not a proof-of-reserve feed.

The structure also introduces a risk vaulted tokens do not carry: basis risk and margin risk. If gold gaps and futures margin is called, the ability to meet that call determines whether the hedge survives. There is no public disclosure of margin buffers, stress testing, or what happens on a failed margin call.

#### Fees, revenue and redemption

Minting is permissioned and available only to KYC-approved counterparties, executed through signed orders via the `Controller`. Settlement is atomic and takes roughly 30 seconds, with no reported minimum transaction size and a 0% mint fee by default, subject to change with market conditions. Economically, minting delivers USDC to the protocol, which posts margin and adds futures exposure, so issuance is constrained only by the depth of the CME gold futures market. That makes it the one genuinely scalable issuance model here: no sourcing, refining, shipping or vaulting of metal.

Redemption is in USD stablecoin, takes roughly 30 seconds, has no stated minimum and costs 0% by default. One disclosed term deserves emphasis: off-market-hours redemptions carry a higher fee set dynamically according to the asset's recent volatility. That is honest and economically coherent, since the protocol cannot adjust its CME hedge when the exchange is closed and must price that risk. It also means the redemption cost is not knowable in advance and is set unilaterally by the issuer. The 0% headline holds only during CME trading hours. Eligibility beyond "KYC-approved counterparties" is not disclosed, so a retail-scale holder acquiring tGLD on Uniswap has no documented path to redeem at all, and would depend on secondary liquidity that is currently near zero.

Tenbin charges holders nothing, ongoing or upfront, and does not need to, because the collateral itself is productive. USDC earns a stablecoin rate and the futures position earns or pays basis. The protocol takes its cut from that yield stream through a `RevenueModule` before passing the remainder to stakers. The asset pays for its own custody.

#### Liquidity and distribution

About 258 tGLD are outstanding at roughly $1.05M market capitalisation and about $4,067, tracking the gold ounce. The only public market is a Uniswap V4 tGLD/USDC pool on Ethereum, which turned over $10.35 in 24 hours and was flagged as inactive with no trades in the preceding three hours. Secondary liquidity is effectively zero and aggregator prices are stale by hours or days. tGLD trades at gold because the KYC-gated, zero-fee, 30-second mint and redeem channel is a far tighter arbitrage rail than any decentralised pool, so institutional participants have no reason to let a premium or discount persist. Another protocol where a premium cannot exist, here because primary redemption is instant and free rather than because the market is deep.

There is no retail distribution of any kind: no savings plan, no recurring purchase, no referral, no affiliate, no agent network, no consumer app. Tenbin is not merely lacking retail features. It is architecturally institutional, gating mint and redeem behind KYC counterparty approval.

Its distribution strategy is decentralised finance composability and institutional credit, a genuinely different channel from anything else here. Prime brokers and market makers include Hidden Road, Ripple Prime and StoneX. On 22 June 2026 the quantitative trading firm JPEG Trading launched a real-world asset curation business with tGLD as its first curated asset, in vaults on Euler. JPEG stated it will hold tGLD on its own balance sheet, on the principle that "the strongest underwriting comes from curators who are willing to stand behind their analysis with their own capital". The vault targets gold-denominated yields of 4% to 6%. The strategy is to make tGLD useful as collateral rather than attractive as a holding.

Two other dated developments. On 18 May 2026 Tenbin deprecated LayerZero and migrated to Chainlink CCIP exclusively for tGLD, tBRL and tMXN following an internal security audit prompted by industry cross-chain incidents, citing CCIP's 16 independent node operators and SOC 2 Type 2 certification. tGLD went live on Ethereum mainnet in private beta on 10 February 2026. No security incidents, exploits or enforcement actions were found, and the LayerZero migration was precautionary rather than a response to a Tenbin breach.

#### Relevance to Aurumix

Tenbin is the structural opposite of Aurumix on almost every axis: institutional not retail, synthetic not physical, BVI not UAE, composable not permissioned, and with no distribution network at all. It is not a competitor for the same customer. Its value to this engagement is that it answers the hardest open question by demonstrating a mechanism Aurumix cannot copy, and the reason it cannot copy it is the most useful thing in this profile.

**Tenbin explains why every well-documented bullion-backed protocol pays zero yield.** It pays a genuine 4% to 6% gold-denominated yield, funded by CME futures basis plus stablecoin rates on its USDC collateral. It can do that precisely because its collateral is USDC and futures margin rather than bullion. Vaulted gold yields nothing and costs money to store. A bar in a vault produces no cash flow, so a physically-backed issuer has only three places to find a dividend: the holders' own fees, an external counterparty who borrows the metal, or the issuer's operating profit. Tenbin's collateral is productive by construction, which is exactly what a bar is not.

That is the arithmetic identity Aurumix has to confront. Aurumix's proposition is that 100% of every dollar buys physical LBMA gold. Aurumix therefore cannot have both "100% physical gold" and "a yield funded by the asset itself". This is not a design gap to engineer around. The realistic options:

- **Encumber the gold through leasing** and accept lessee counterparty risk, with the AgaBullion default of 26 January 2026 as the live illustration of what "title remains with the lessor" resolves to in practice.
- **Fund the dividend from operating profit** and accept the securities-classification exposure and the weaker value story.
- **Hold a portion of reserves in a yield-bearing form** and stop claiming 100% physical.

Tenbin is also the correct benchmark for testing whether a yield promise is fundable. Unlike protocols advertising multiples of what they deliver, Tenbin's disclosed source is structurally capable of paying what it advertises: stablecoin rates and futures basis are real, external, market-sized yield streams that do not come from other investors' fees. That is what a fundable yield promise looks like, and it is the standard the ICS Dividend should be measured against. One caveat: the 4% to 6% figure comes from a partner's launch announcement rather than realised performance, and the vault launched only weeks ago.

Three further points:

- **A third token-standard option Aurumix has not considered.** Aurumix is weighing a permissioned ERC-3643 base plus an ERC-20 wrapper. Tenbin does something cheaper: a plain, freely transferable, non-upgradeable ERC-20, with permissioning enforced only at the mint and redeem boundary through a Controller and a RestrictedRegistry, plus an admin power to burn a restricted holder's balance. The token composes anywhere while the issuer still controls who enters and exits the primary market. ICS standing, dividend entitlement and credit eligibility could be tracked in a registry contract keyed to holder identity rather than baked into transfer restrictions. It enforces less than ERC-3643 does, since an anonymous buyer holds a token carrying no entitlements, which creates a two-tier market and a mispricing risk. It is also far cheaper to build and integrate. Flagging the tradeoff, not recommending it.
- **Alignment between the backing claim and the exit mechanism.** Tenbin offers no physical redemption and does not claim physical backing, so its story is internally consistent. Aurumix offers no physical redemption while making a stronger backing claim than its exit terms support. The gap is closable from either end, and closing it from the disclosure end is free.
- **A workable template for buyback terms.** Tenbin's volatility-scaled off-hours redemption fee addresses the same problem Aurumix's cash buyback faces: honouring exits when the issuer cannot transact in the underlying. Charge for it, disclose that you charge for it, and set it dynamically.

Two closing notes. Tenbin is the sharpest "audited is not attested" example in this set: six or more audit firms across two rounds, and zero reserve attestation. Aurumix should expect that conflation to be the industry norm and can differentiate cheaply by publishing both separately and labelling them clearly. On wind-down, Tenbin comes closer than most by accident. The SPC structure implies portfolio-level segregation on insolvency, and it never publishes the constitutional documents that would let a holder rely on it. Having a protective structure and being able to prove you have one are different things. The cheap differentiation is publishing the document, not merely having the structure.
