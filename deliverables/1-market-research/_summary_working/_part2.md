#### What it is

PAXG is a gold-denominated token issued by Paxos Trust Company, N.A. Each token represents one fine troy ounce of a specific, serial-numbered London Good Delivery bar held in LBMA-approved vaults in London. It launched in September 2019 and is the largest tokenized-gold product other than Tether Gold.

The product is deliberately narrow: a custody receipt with an onchain wrapper, and nothing else. No savings plan, no yield, no tiering, no credit facility, no referral network. Everything Aurumix layers on top of a gold core, PAXG has consciously omitted, which is exactly why it is the useful comparison. PAXG is what the regulated-minimum version of this product looks like, and it has reached ~US$1.79bn doing only that.

Minimum purchase is 0.03 PAXG, roughly US$120, driven by a 0.02 PAXG internal conversion cost. The token divides to 18 decimals and there is no minimum holding. So the minting minimum sits well above Aurumix's US$20 SIP floor while the holding minimum is effectively zero, and small buyers are expected to acquire PAXG on the secondary market rather than mint it.

#### Legal structure and regulatory standing

This is the strongest disclosure of any protocol in the set, and the language is unusually precise. The PAXG Terms and Conditions state that "your PAXG tokens are akin to a warehouse receipt representing your beneficial ownership of a pro rata portion of Allocated Gold."

Three things follow. The holder has beneficial ownership rather than legal title: Paxos holds the bars, the holder has a beneficial interest in a pro rata share of the allocated pool, and Paxos expressly reserves the right to reallocate which specific bars back which holders "for operational efficiency," so the serial number a holder looks up is real but not permanently theirs. The gold is allocated rather than unallocated: the terms define Allocated Gold as "a specific gold bar held in an LBMA-approved security carrier vault, identifiable by a unique serial number, weighting and purity percentage," and define Unallocated Gold separately as "a liability of such institution whereby the holder of unallocated gold owns a claim to an equivalent balance of gold." PAXG is backed by the former. That is the distinction most gold tokens blur and Paxos does not.

Bankruptcy remoteness is claimed but asymmetrically documented. The Paxos blog asserts that Paxos is "required to hold all customer funds in segregated, bankruptcy remote accounts" and that "your gold bars will always be yours, even in the unlikely case something happens to Paxos." That is a marketing page. The terms contain no Paxos-insolvency clause at all. They do contain a detailed clause disclaiming liability for a Clearing Bank insolvency ("Paxos is not responsible for any losses associated with any default by a Clearing Bank... including but not limited to, insolvency, default, or operational error") and nothing comparable governing the failure of Paxos Trust Company itself. The marketing makes a promise the legal document does not restate, which is worth naming precisely because Paxos is otherwise the best-documented issuer here. The counterweight is that the OCC national trust charter is itself the substantive protection: a national trust bank holds fiduciary assets off its own balance sheet by operation of banking law, a stronger structural claim than any contract clause. Paxos relies on the charter rather than on drafting.

The regulator is the OCC. Paxos filed on 11 August 2025 to convert its NYDFS limited-purpose trust charter to a national trust charter. On 12 December 2025 the OCC conditionally approved five national trust bank charters, including the conversion of Paxos Trust Company into an uninsured national trust bank. The other four were Ripple National Trust Bank and First National Digital Currency Bank as de novo charters, plus BitGo Bank & Trust, N.A. and Fidelity Digital Assets, N.A. as conversions. Paxos completed its conversion the same day and states that "all of Paxos Trust Company's US-based activity will be subject to OCC supervision." The entity is now styled Paxos Trust Company, N.A., and the PAXG terms now say Paxos Trust issues PAXG pursuant to specific approval from the OCC. No public charter number was found on the OCC release or on Paxos pages.

One inconsistency in the issuer's own disclosure is worth recording. The Paxos PAXG transparency page still describes NYDFS as the body approving the choice of auditor. Paxos has not uniformly updated its own site after the conversion, so some issuer pages, and effectively all third-party coverage dated before December 2025, still name the wrong regulator. That is a disclosure hygiene point with a practical lesson: an issuer that is otherwise best-in-class at legal drafting still left a stale regulator name on a live transparency page, and diligence finds that kind of thing.

No VARA, ADGM, DFSA, MAS or EU MiCA authorisation for PAXG was located. It is a purely US-chartered product distributed globally through exchanges.

#### Custody and reserve verification

Paxos Trust Company, N.A. is the legal custodian. The vault operator is Brink's in London, named on Paxos's own blog: "we store gold reserves with Brink's bullion vaults in London." The PAXG product page says only "LBMA vaults in London," so the operator name sits on a blog post rather than in the terms. Gold is allocated to specific serial-numbered LBMA Good Delivery bars, with a contractual right for Paxos to reallocate.

Paxos is one of the few issuers that does reserve verification and contract audit both, without conflating them in its own copy. The reserve attestation is monthly, by KPMG LLP since February 2025 and by WithumSmith+Brown before that, under AICPA attestation standards, published at the PAXG transparency page as a dated time series running through June 2026. Separately, the Ethereum contract was audited before launch and the Solana deployment was audited by Zellic, although no PAXG-specific audit PDF was located on paxos.com. One secondary source claims an annual physical bar audit by Bureau Veritas; that could not be confirmed against Paxos or Bureau Veritas primary sources and should be treated as unverified.

The honest gap is that a per-address lookup is not a bar list. A holder can enter their Ethereum address and see the serial number, value and physical characteristics of their bars, but there is no full public list of all bars backing total supply, so a holder can verify their own slice and cannot independently sum the pool. Total-supply verification still depends on trusting KPMG's monthly snapshot. Between snapshots there is no continuous onchain proof, and no Chainlink or other proof-of-reserve oracle for PAXG was found.

#### Fees, revenue and redemption

| Fee line | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Creation (mint) | Tiered by order size: 0.02 PAXG flat for 0.03–2 PAXG; 1.000% (2–25); 0.750% (25–50); 0.500% (50–75); 0.250% (75–200); 0.150% (200–800); 0.125% (800+) | Currently waived under a promotion. The end date renders on the fee page as "09/31/26", a date that does not exist |
| Destruction (redeem) | Same tiered schedule, not waived | Applies through the Paxos wallet, not on exchange order-book trades |
| Storage / custody | US$0.00 | "Paxos does not charge gold storage fees to its customers at this time." The terms reserve the right to start |
| Onchain transfer fee | Contract supports a fee, historically 0.02%, currently understood to be set to zero | Users pay gas only |
| Inactivity fee | US$2.00 per month | After 12 months with a non-zero balance and no issuance or redemption activity |
| Banking fees | Passed through at cost | |
| Physical bar delivery fee | Stated only in the Paxos User Guide, not on the public fee page | Secondary sources cite US$20 domestic and US$30 international, unconfirmed |

PAXG's answer to the custody-fee problem is to charge nothing recurring and recover cost elsewhere. Ongoing storage is zero. Revenue comes from transaction events, meaning destruction fees on redemption, creation fees once the promotion lapses and the US$2 inactivity fee, plus cross-subsidy from a much larger stablecoin and infrastructure business. PAXG does not have to pay for itself.

The terms document the fallback, and it is directly relevant to Aurumix. If Paxos ever does charge storage it will do so like this: "Paxos may charge storage fees to all token holders by issuing to Paxos new PAXG tokens, thereby diluting the value of existing PAXG tokens. The storage fee will be in line with industry practice, and such storage fee will be passed on to all PAXG token holders on a pro rata basis." Paxos's peg is one token equals one ounce, so minting against an unchanged gold pool breaks the 1:1 claim: after a dilution event one PAXG is backed by slightly less than one ounce. Paxos has reserved the right to break its own headline promise, disclosed it in the terms, and never used it.

Redemption gives PAXG the most complete exit stack in this set: to USD at market at any time through a Paxos account, to Unallocated Gold by wire to a bullion account (framed as institutional), or to physical Allocated Gold at a minimum of 430 PAXG per bar plus the User Guide fee. USD conversions round down to the nearest 1/100th of a troy ounce. At ~US$4,027 an ounce a 430 PAXG bar redemption is roughly US$1.73m, so physical delivery is real and structurally out of reach for retail. It is a credibility mechanism for the peg, not a consumer feature. Paxos also pushes delivery risk onto the holder: "you are responsible for delivery and once your bars are in the possession of the delivery service you choose, then Paxos will have been deemed to have fulfilled its obligation for delivery."

One asymmetry deserves emphasis. The terms give Paxos sweeping discretionary power over both token and metal, in capitals: "WE MAY FREEZE, TEMPORARILY OR PERMANENTLY, YOUR USE OF, AND ACCESS TO, PAXG OR THE LONDON GOOD DELIVERY GOLD BARS BACKING YOUR PAXG, WITH OR WITHOUT ADVANCE NOTICE." On illegality, PAXG "AND THE ASSETS BACKING SUCH PAX MAY BE FORFEITED" and may "BECOME WHOLLY AND PERMANENTLY UNRECOVERABLE AND UNUSABLE." The beneficial-ownership claim is real and conditional on Paxos's compliance discretion, and the contract's asset protection role, able to freeze accounts and wipe balances, is what makes that language enforceable onchain.

#### Liquidity and distribution

As at 28 July 2026: price ~US$4,027 per token, market cap ~US$1.79bn, 24h volume ~US$105.6m, circulating supply ~444,808 tokens (~13.8 tonnes). The all-time high of US$5,619.09 in January 2026 is a gold-price artefact, not a premium event.

Volume to AUM runs at roughly 5.9% a day. PAXG turns over more value in a day than PGOLD holds in total. It is the most liquid tokenized gold product with a genuine physical redemption channel, and it trades at or extremely near net asset value rather than at a 3–8% premium. That is not an accident. Deep liquidity plus a credible, if high-minimum, arbitrage path is exactly what destroys a premium: any holder of 430 PAXG or more can mint away a persistent premium.

Distribution runs direct through paxos.com and the Paxos wallet under KYC, plus broad third-party exchange listings (Binance, Kraken, Coinbase, Gemini, Backpack, BitMart and others), and now Solana DeFi through Sunrise DeFi. The target is self-directed crypto-native investors and institutions seeking gold exposure, and marketing leans on cost comparison against ETFs and physical dealers. Geography is global via exchanges, with direct minting constrained by Paxos's US-chartered KYC perimeter. No India, UAE or NRI-specific channel was found.

No savings plan, recurring purchase or premium schedule is documented, and no referral, affiliate or commission-based distribution of any kind was found. One feature runs directly against Aurumix's customer profile: PAXG charges US$2 per month on dormant balances, actively penalising the buy-and-hold, low-engagement saver.

Recent developments. PAXG launched on Solana around 25 June 2026 using the Token-2022 standard, with Sunrise DeFi as ecosystem partner, LayerZero Stargate for bridging from Ethereum and Zellic as smart contract auditor. A "Portal"-wrapped PAXG also exists on Solana and should not be confused with the Paxos issuance. KPMG replaced WithumSmith+Brown as attestor in February 2025. Across an 18-month review, no PAXG depeg, security incident, exploit, lawsuit or enforcement action was identified, no change to custody arrangements was found, and Paxos launched no competing gold token.

#### Relevance to Aurumix

Two framing points first. PAXG is issued by Paxos Trust Company, N.A. under an OCC national trust bank charter granted on 12 December 2025, so it is an OCC-supervised product and NYDFS is only the prior charter. Paxos's own transparency page still refers to NYDFS as the body approving its auditor, a live example of disclosure lagging corporate change, and it means most third-party coverage in circulation names the wrong regulator. Second, PAXG is no longer Ethereum-only: it has been live on Solana since June 2026.

**Custody fee: PAXG gives Aurumix its answer, and it is not the obvious one.** PAXG charges zero ongoing custody fee and funds the vault from transaction fees plus cross-subsidy from a far larger stablecoin business. Aurumix has no such adjacent business, so straight imitation is off the table. The transferable asset is the fallback Paxos drafted: charge storage by minting new tokens to the issuer and diluting holders pro rata.

For Paxos that would break a one-token-equals-one-ounce peg, which is why it has never been used. For Aurumix, whose price is grams divided by tokens, dilution is peg-neutral by construction: mint to treasury, price per token falls by the fee, and the grams-to-tokens identity survives untouched. The same mechanism is peg-breaking for Paxos and peg-compatible for Aurumix. It needs no cash collection from a US$20-per-month saver, it reaches holders who never log in, and it is enforceable against holders who never interact with the platform. There is now an OCC-supervised, KPMG-attested issuer with the clause on the page. Adopt the mechanism and copy the disclosure discipline: state the rate, state that it is charged by issuance, commit to advance notice.

**Dividend: PAXG pays nothing, and that is the evidence.** The largest, best-regulated, most liquid gold token in the market at US$1.79bn AUM offers zero yield and grew anyway. Its gold sits allocated at Brink's, unencumbered, doing nothing. Gold-token AUM does not require a yield to accumulate, and the issuer with the most regulatory latitude to construct one has declined to. Paxos, an OCC-supervised trust bank, will not lend or lease customer gold to manufacture a return, which says something about how a serious prudential regulator views encumbering allocated client metal. If Aurumix needs the ICS Dividend, PAXG offers no funding template. It demonstrates the opposite: the compliant path is to promise no yield at all.

**Premium and liquidity: PAXG is the counter-evidence to the 3–8% premium thesis.** US$1.79bn AUM against US$105.6m daily volume (~5.9% turnover), a genuine mint-and-redeem arbitrage channel, and it trades at par. PGOLD is the illiquid extreme, PAXG is the liquid extreme, and neither sustains a premium. Deep liquidity plus open arbitrage equals net asset value. Aurumix's closed redemption is the only thing that could hold a premium, which means the premium would be a product of the exit restriction rather than of demand. Say that to the client plainly before it is built into revenue.

**Proof of reserve: PAXG sets the retail benchmark.** Monthly KPMG attestation under AICPA standards published as a dated time series, a per-address bar lookup showing serial number and physical characteristics, and a separately audited smart contract. Assume a sophisticated retail buyer or agent has seen this. Match the monthly cadence and the credential tier, and keep attestation and contract audit visually and verbally separate. PAXG's own gap is the opening: no full public bar list and no onchain proof-of-reserve oracle. Publishing a complete bar list or a live proof-of-reserve feed would put Aurumix ahead of the category leader on one dimension at modest cost.

**Redemption: physical redemption can be real and still be retail-irrelevant.** The 430 PAXG minimum is ~US$1.73m per bar. It functions as an arbitrage backstop and a marketing proof point. Aurumix should stop treating "no physical redemption" as a pure weakness, because PAXG's physical redemption is equally unavailable to a US$75-per-month saver. The difference is that Paxos can say "redeemable for physical gold" in a headline. If Aurumix wants that headline without the operational burden, a high-minimum, fee-bearing physical option deliberately priced out of retail reach is the industry-standard construction. If it stays with cash-only buyback, it must not claim "you own physical gold" without qualification. Paxos earns that claim with allocated serial numbers and a real, if remote, delivery path.

**Token standard: PAXG's plain ERC-20 is a consequence of having no holder state.** Paxos got composability, exchange listings and the resulting liquidity by making the token anonymous and stateless, then retrofitted freeze-and-wipe admin powers to regain compliance control. It wanted permissioned control without a permissioned standard. Aurumix cannot copy this, because ICS tier, dividend entitlement, credit standing and buyback rights are all holder state that an anonymous DEX transfer destroys. The supporting signal for the ERC-3643 lean is Paxos's newest deployment: on Solana it chose Token-2022 with native compliance extensions rather than the barest standard available. Given the choice today, the most conservative issuer in the category picks the compliance-native token.

**Regulatory route: PAXG is not a UAE comparable and should not be used as one.** No VARA, ADGM, DFSA or MiCA authorisation was found. It is a US national trust bank product distributed globally through exchange listings. The transferable insight is structural: Paxos put the entity under a prudential fiduciary regulator and kept the product deliberately simple, so the token needed no separate securities analysis. Aurumix's hybrid of gold core plus dividend plus credit is what forces dual regulation. The Paxos precedent argues for severability: licence the gold core cleanly as an ARVA and place the dividend and credit facility in a separate, separately regulated entity, rather than seeking one licence covering a hybrid.

**Distribution: the gap is real and it is Aurumix's actual differentiator.** No SIP, no recurring buy, no referral, no affiliate, no agent tier, plus a US$2 per month inactivity fee that penalises exactly the dormant retail saver Aurumix courts. Nothing at the credible end of this market is competing for the monthly-contribution customer. The flip side is that Paxos reached US$1.79bn without any distribution machinery at all, by being the compliance-credible default that exchanges list. Two different businesses: Paxos let distribution come to it, Aurumix must go and buy it.

**Wind-down: better than any peer, and still not a plan.** Paxos's marketing asserts segregated, bankruptcy-remote accounts and that gold bars remain the holder's even if something happens to Paxos, and the OCC trust charter gives that real structural force. But the terms contain no issuer-insolvency clause, only a Clearing Bank default disclaimer, and no published wind-down procedure exists: no trustee, no sequence, no distribution mechanic. Even the best-documented issuer in the category has not filled this gap, and Aurumix can take it cheaply.

Takeaways for Aurumix:

- Adopt the Paxos dilution mechanism for the custody fee. It is peg-neutral under a grams-divided-by-tokens formula and there is now an OCC-supervised precedent for the drafting.
- Match monthly Big Four attestation as the retail benchmark, then beat it with a full bar list or a live proof-of-reserve feed.
- Treat zero yield as the compliant default. The most regulated issuer in the category refuses to encumber client metal to manufacture a return.
- Consider severability: gold core as an ARVA under VARA, dividend and credit in a separate regulated entity, following Paxos's entity-regulated, product-simple structure.
- Never build in a dormancy penalty. The US$2 inactivity fee is the clearest signal that PAXG is not built for the monthly saver, and that customer is unoccupied ground.
- Describe regulatory status with precision and keep every page current. Paxos's stale NYDFS reference on a live transparency page is the kind of inconsistency diligence surfaces.
