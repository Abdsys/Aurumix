# How nine gold businesses make their money (worked examples)

**Price basis.** Gold was USD 4,055/oz at 9:11 ET on 28 Oct 2026 [H] ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/current-price-of-gold-10-28-2026/)). That works out to:
- USD 130.4/g
- AED 478.8/g for 24K and AED 438.6/g for 22K, at the 3.6725 peg [H, derived]
- India at ₹88/USD plus 6% import duty: about ₹12,160/g for 24K and ₹11,140/g for 22K [M, derived]

Tags: [H] primary source, [M] reputable secondary source or my maths on disclosed figures, [L] estimate.

---

## 1. Gold loans (pawn-style lending)

**What happens, step by step**
1. The borrower brings jewellery to a branch. An appraiser tests the purity, weighs it and takes off the weight of any stones.
2. The lender values only the gold. Making charges get no value [L] ([GoldRatesInDubai](https://goldratesindubai.com/news/gold-loan-dubai)).
3. RBI caps how much can be lent against that value:
   - 85% for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh
   - 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh
   - 75% above ₹5 lakh [H] ([Manappuram](https://www.manappuram.com/gold-loan-interest-rates))
4. Muthoot aims for about 57% on average [M] ([Perivis](https://blog.perivis.com/2026/05/20/muthoot-finance-sees-record-gold-loan-aum-skips-margin-guidance/)).
5. Cash goes out in minutes. The gold is sealed, insured and kept in the branch strong room.
6. The borrower repays the loan plus interest and gets the gold back.
7. If they don't repay, the lender auctions the gold, takes what it is owed and returns any surplus. Muthoot booked about ₹50 cr of auction income in Q4 FY26 [M].

**Where the margin comes from:** the lender charges 9.9–23.3% a year [H] (Manappuram) and borrows at bank or bond rates. Losses are tiny because the gold is worth more than the loan. Muthoot made a profit (PAT) of ₹10,134 cr on ₹1.65 lakh cr of gold-loan AUM. That is about 6% of year-end AUM [M].

**UAE:** banks charge 8–14% a year and lend 70–80% of value [L].

**Worked example: 20g of 22K = ₹2,22,800**

| Line | ₹ | % of loan |
|---|---|---|
| Loan at 75% LTV | 1,67,100 (USD 1,900) | |
| Interest at 20% for a year [L] | +33,420 | 20.0 |
| Funding cost (about 80% of the loan is borrowed at ~9.3%) [L] | −12,530 | 7.5 |
| Branch running costs [L] | −5,010 | 3.0 |
| Credit losses [L] | −840 | 0.5 |
| **Pre-tax profit** | **15,040** | **9.0** (about 6.7% after tax) |

**What still protects the lender if the borrower walks away:** the loan plus a year's interest is about 90% of the gold's value. The auction covers the debt unless gold falls more than about 10%.

## 2. Jewellery making charges

**How a Dubai jeweller builds the price**
- Weight × the day's rate for that karat (the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group publishes the rate), plus the making charge, plus 5% VAT on the whole amount [H] ([PBZ](https://www.pbz.ae/insights/vat-for-gold-and-jewellery-traders-uae-reverse-charge)).
- Making charges [M] ([GoldRatesInDubai](https://goldratesindubai.com/dubai-gold-making-charges)):
  - machine-made chains and bangles: 3–8%
  - standard pieces: 8–15%
  - handmade: 15–25%

**India**
- 3% GST on the whole bill [M].
- Making charges at the big brands [M] ([Gullak](https://gullak.money/blog/jewellery-making-charges/)):
  - Tanishq chains: 9–25%
  - Kalyan chains: 19–36%
  - Malabar chains: 13–19%

**What the making charge pays for**
- The craftsman's labour and gold lost in melting [L]
- Hallmarking
- Shop rent and staff
- Interest on gold held as stock
- Marketing
- The jeweller's profit. Titan's jewellery EBIT was about 11% of sales [M]. I could not re-open that source.

**Buyback:** the jeweller pays for gold content only. The making charge and VAT are never returned [M] ([Himmath](https://himmathgold.com/how-to-sell-gold-in-dubai-buyback-rates-spreads-and-what-youll-actually-be-paid/)). The jeweller then melts the old piece or reuses the gold.

**Worked example: 20g 22K bangle, Dubai**

| Line | AED |
|---|---|
| Gold, 20g × 438.6 | 8,771 |
| Making charge at AED 40/g (about 9%) | 800 |
| VAT at 5% on 9,571 | 479 |
| **Customer pays** | **10,050** |
| Sold back next week, gold at about −2% [L] | ~8,600 |
| **Customer loss** | **~1,450 (14%)** |

**About Jar's "half of revenue" figure:** Jar's Nek jewellery line brought in more than ₹100 cr, and Jar's operating revenue was ₹208 cr ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/indian-fintech-jar-turns-profitable-by-helping-millions-save-in-gold/)). The Nek figure is probably the full jewellery bill, with the gold included. If so, "half" overstates it. Jar's actual margin is the making-charge slice, roughly ₹10–25 cr [L].

## 3. Small gift bars and coins (1–10g)

**What happens, step by step**
1. A refiner (PAMP, Valcambi, Emirates Gold) rolls 999.9 gold into strips.
2. It cuts blanks, stamps or mints them, assays them, and seals each bar in a serial-numbered card.
3. It sells to wholesalers at a small fabrication premium.
4. The retailer adds its markup.

In the UAE, gold of 99% purity or more is zero-rated for VAT [H] (PBZ).

**Why small bars carry a high % premium:** the card, assay, packaging, stock and staff time cost about the same per piece whether the bar is 1g or 10g. That fixed cost is a big share of a 1g bar.

Typical premiums at US dealers [M] ([FindBullionPrices](https://new.findbullionprices.com/blog/common-gold-bars-sizes-weights-new-investors/)):

| Size | Premium over spot |
|---|---|
| 1g | 15–30% |
| 10g | 5–10% |
| 1oz | 2–5% |

**Who earns:** mostly the retailer. Emirates Minting's own making charge on a 1g bar is only AED 12.60, about 2.6% [H] ([Emirates Minting](https://emiratesminting.ae/product/E27461532M-1-gram-gold-bar/gold/gold-bar)).

**Buyback:** dealers pay melt value minus about 1% [M] (Himmath). The customer loses the whole premium.

**Worked example: 1g bar**
- Gold AED 479 + mint charge 12.60 = **AED 491 factory cost**
- Retail AED 585 [L] ([Al Fanan](https://www.alfanan.ae/collections/24k-gold-bar)), so the **retailer adds about AED 94 (19%)**
- Dubai shops ask up to AED 786 for some branded 1g bars [L]
- Sold back at about AED 474, so the **customer loses about AED 111 (19%)**
- For a 10g bar, the loss is about 6–10%

## 4. Retail digital-gold buy/sell spread

**What happens, step by step**
1. The app (PhonePe, Jar, Botim) is only the shop front. A backend company (SafeGold, MMTC-PAMP, Augmont, OGold) is the legal seller.
2. The backend takes a live wholesale price feed and adds a spread to get the buy price. It subtracts a spread to get the sell price.
3. In India, 3% GST goes on top of each purchase. In the UAE, investment gold has no VAT [H].
4. The customer's account shows grams straight away.
5. The backend nets buys against sells across all its partner apps. It keeps a stock of bars in a vault under a trustee and buys more kilobars from refiners when net buying builds up [L].
6. Gold bought from refiners and custodians was 99.2% of SafeGold's spending [H] ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/safegold-clocks-rs-6867-cr-in-gold-transactions-in-fy25-turns-ebitda-positive-10814304)).

**Spreads in India:** 2–5% [M], or about 4–6% at PhonePe [M].

**Worked example: ₹10,000 bought, sold after a year (India, 3% spread)**

| Line | ₹ |
|---|---|
| GST to the government | 300 |
| Spread at entry | 141 |
| Spread at exit | 141 |
| Redemption fee | 24 |
| **Customer gets back** | **9,401 (about −6%)** |

Source: [OroPocket](https://blog.oropocket.com/digital-gold-charges-in-india-explained-spreads-gst-storage-and-selling-fees/) [M].

**Where the ₹282 of spread goes**
- Backend: about 0.85% of flow, or about ₹80 [H, derived from SafeGold]
- App: about ₹200, after payment costs (UPI is close to free) [L]
- For comparison, Jar's gross margin was about 4.7% of sales [M]

## 5. Card FX on trips home

**What happens when a UAE card is used in India**
1. The Indian merchant charges in rupees.
2. Visa or Mastercard converts INR to AED at the network rate.
3. The UAE bank bills the customer in AED and adds a foreign-transaction fee. Since 22 Sep 2025 that fee is **3.14%**: 1% network plus 2.14% bank [M] ([Fintechnews](https://fintechnews.ae/27096/fintechdubai/uae-foreign-transaction-fee-hike-sept/)).
4. On the merchant side, the Indian merchant pays up to 3% for international cards [H] ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)). Part of that 3% goes back to the UAE bank as interchange, roughly 1–1.8% [L].

**The trap at the till (dynamic currency conversion):** the terminal offers to "pay in AED" at its own rate. That rate can carry a markup such as +2.95%, and sometimes much more [M] ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_currency_conversion)). The customer may still pay the bank's fee on top.

**Worked example: ₹10,000 spend = AED 417.30**

| Who | AED |
|---|---|
| Customer pays | 430.40 |
| UAE bank FX fee (2.14%) | 8.93 |
| Network (1%) | 4.17 |
| Merchant's 3% fee (₹300) | 12.50, split between the Indian gateway and the UAE bank |
| **Total friction** | **about AED 25.60 (6%)** [L] |

**Who keeps most:** the UAE bank, through its FX fee plus a share of interchange, about 3–3.5% of spend [L].

**Two caveats**
- A new card would also pay the 1% network cost, so the room to undercut the banks is at most about 2.14%.
- NRIs can now pay with UPI from NRE/NRO accounts on +971 numbers [M] ([BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/nri/invest/story/now-nris-can-use-upi-in-india-with-their-foreign-mobile-numbers-no-indian-sim-needed-details-here-482712-2025-07-02)). That skips card FX altogether.

## 6. Branded and collector coins (Trump coin, US Mint proofs)

**Why the premium exists**
- Government backing and legal-tender status
- Proof finish: polished dies, several strikes, hand inspection, capsule and box
- Scarcity: limited runs and per-household caps
- Fan or collector demand

**The Trump coins**
- The US Mint's USD 250 Trump proof is 1oz of .9999 gold. It goes on sale on 5 Nov 2026, limited to one per household [M] ([WNN](https://www.worldnumismaticnews.com/2026/09/15/2026-trump-gold-coin-250-proof-november-5/)).
- Its price follows the Mint's weekly grid. At a gold average of USD 4,400–4,449.99 it would cost about USD 5,440 [M] ([CoinNews](https://www.coinnews.net/2026/09/11/mint-reveals-250-trump-gold-coin/)). That is about 23% over melt [M, derived].
- The Trump USD 1 coin contains no gold. It sold at USD 2.44 each in rolls [H] ([CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-1-dollar-coins-us-mint-sale/)).

**Who keeps the premium:** the Mint, plus early resellers while the hype lasts.

**How the premium decays:** dealers buy back at what they can resell quickly. Once the first buyers are served and flippers sell, modern proofs with large runs usually drift back toward melt plus a few percent [L]. Only truly scarce issues hold their premium.

**Worked example (gold flat)**
- Buy at USD 5,440, about USD 1,015 over USD 4,425 melt
- After the hype, the premium falls to 5–8%, so resale is about USD 4,650–4,780 [L]
- **The customer loses about 12–15%** while gold stays flat

## 7. Custody and storage

**What happens**
- Bars sit in a professional vault such as Brink's, Loomis or Malca-Amit.
- "Allocated" storage means specific serial-numbered bars on a bar list. They are the customer's property and stay outside the company's balance sheet if it fails. "Unallocated" means only a claim on the provider.
- The vault operator insures the metal for at least its full value [H] ([BullionVault](https://www.bullionvault.com/help/FAQs/FAQs_vaulting.html)).
- Independent auditors count the bars, check serial numbers and reconcile them to customer records. BullionVault publishes its bar list daily [H].

**Prices**
- BullionVault: 0.12%/yr, insurance included [H]
- Glint: 0.24%/yr [M]
- Goldmoney: 0.25–0.5%/yr with a USD 10/month minimum [M]
- PAXG, XAUT and Kinesis: 0% [M]

**Why it is priced near zero**
- Competitors charge nothing.
- At wholesale scale, vault and insurance cost only about 0.03–0.10%/yr [L].
- Apps recover it through the spread.
- A visible fee on a small balance drives users away.

**Worked example**
- A USD 1,500 balance at 0.12% pays **USD 1.80/yr**.
- Goldmoney's USD 120/yr minimum would be 8% of that balance. That is why minimums exist and why the model only suits large accounts.
- A USD 50m book at 0.12% earns USD 60k a year. Vault and insurance (USD 15–50k) plus an audit (about USD 30k) leave roughly zero [L].

## 8. B2B gold infrastructure (white-label digital gold)

**What happens**
1. A partner app (PhonePe, e& money, Botim) shows a gold screen that runs on a backend API.
2. The backend (SafeGold, Augmont, OGold) is the legal seller. It:
   - quotes the price
   - buys from refiners
   - allocates gold in a vault under a trustee
   - handles delivery, buyback and compliance
3. The partner earns a commission on each buy, sell, SIP payment and redemption, plus volume incentives [M] ([Chirayu](http://blog.chirayusoft.com/2025/11/how-to-tie-up-with-augmont-as-partner.html)).
4. Partner apps also buy gold back 1.5–3% below the IBJA rate (the Indian bullion association's daily benchmark) [L].

**Why margins are thin**
- The partner owns the customer and can switch supplier.
- The product is the same everywhere.
- The backend carries the stock, vault, insurance, audit, technology and delivery costs.
- Only a few suppliers exist, and they compete on price.

**Worked example: SafeGold FY25, per ₹100 of gold sold** [H, derived from Entrackr]

| Line | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Sale | 100.00 |
| Gold cost | −99.15 |
| **Gross margin** | **0.85** |
| Staff | −0.18 |
| Other expenses | −0.45 |
| Other items | ~−0.19 |
| **EBITDA** | **0.03** |

On ₹6,867 cr of sales, SafeGold posted a net loss of ₹12.2 cr.

**UAE:** SafeGold already powers e& money (Apr 2026), and OGold powers Botim (Aug 2025) [M].

## 9. Token issuance (PAXG, XAUT)

**Mint and redeem flow**
1. A verified institution sends USD. XAUT direct purchase needs at least 50 XAUT, about USD 200k [M].
2. The issuer buys allocated London Good Delivery bars, held at Brink's London for PAXG and in Switzerland for XAUT.
3. It mints one token per fine troy ounce.
4. The institution sells the tokens to retail buyers on exchanges.
5. To redeem, the tokens are returned and burned. The holder gets USD, or for XAUT a whole bar of about 430oz [M].
6. Auditors confirm that bars match supply; Paxos publishes monthly [M].

**Fees**
- PAXG: creation fee waived until 1 Jan 2027. Redemption runs 0.125–0.5% in bands. No storage fee [H] ([TokenizedWire](https://tokenizedwire.com/explainers/paxg-redemption-fees/)).
- XAUT: 0.25% each way [M].

**Why the issuer earns little**
- Fees apply only when tokens are created or redeemed, which is a small flow next to the total supply.
- No storage fee is charged, yet the issuer pays vault, insurance, audit and legal costs every year.
- Exchanges capture the trading revenue.
- XAUT's supply is about USD 3bn, yet it earns only about USD 3–6m a year in fees [L].
- CACHE Gold shut down in 2025 because its margins were too thin [M].

**Worked example: USD 1m minted**

| Line | USD |
|---|---|
| XAUT mint fee (0.25%) | +2,500 |
| PAXG mint fee (waived) | 0 |
| PAXG redemption fee | +1,250 |
| Issuer's yearly holding cost (0.05–0.10%) [L] | −500 to −1,000 every year |

One XAUT mint fee covers about 2.5–5 years of holding cost. After that, the token loses money for the issuer unless it is redeemed.
