**Short answer**
- Yes. Some companies earn money by leasing out the gold they hold. It is a small business, and the return mostly comes from lending to jewellers. Gold itself earns nothing. (M)
- In normal markets the wholesale gold lease rate is well under 1% a year. (M) The 2-4% "gold yields" offered to retail customers are mostly a credit spread for lending metal to smaller, riskier jewellers. (M)
- The biggest regulated gold tokens (PAXG, Matrixdock XAUm, Tether XAUT) earn nothing on their gold and choose not to lease it. (H)
- The best-documented retail leasing business, Monetary Metals, had a borrower default in January 2026. (H)

### 1. How gold leasing works
- **The deal.** The owner (a central bank, a vault holder or a platform) lends metal for a fee, paid in gold or cash. Title usually passes to the borrower. The owner is left with a promise to get the same weight back. (H)
- **Who borrows, and why:**
  - **Jewellers and fabricators** borrow metal as stock ("gold metal loan" or consignment) and repay from sales. This means they never have to own the price risk on their inventory. (H)
  - **Refiners** borrow to fund metal sitting in the refining pipeline. (H)
  - **Bullion banks** borrow from central banks to lend on, to fund short positions or to run arbitrage trades. (H)
  - **Miners** borrowed gold and sold it forward to hedge in the 1990s and 2000s. Most of that has been unwound. (M)
- **Collateral.** Cash, standby letters of credit, guarantees, and "jeweller's block" insurance (cover on a jeweller's stock). Monetary Metals' filed deal with Streamex requires block insurance at 110% of the leased value, plus a second Lloyd's layer at 110% that covers lessee fraud and unexplained loss ([SEC Ex. 10.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1530766/000164117225026853/ex10-1.htm)). (H) Monetary Metals also takes corporate guarantees, UCC filings (US security-interest registrations) and sometimes personal guarantees ([Monetary Metals](https://www.monetary-metals.com/how-we-protect-client-gold-and-silver/)). (H)
- **Tenor.** Usually 1-12 months. Jeweller loans often run 90-180 days. (M)
- **Pricing.** Lease rate ≈ the USD interest rate minus the gold forward premium (GOFO). The LBMA stopped publishing GOFO in January 2015, so rates now come from dealer quotes. (M)

### 4. Risks
- **Borrower default.** Indian banks lost heavily on gold loans to jewellers: Winsome Diamonds (2012-13, about ₹6,800 cr) and Kanishk Gold (2018, ₹824 cr). (M) Jewellery stock is weak collateral because the borrower sells it in the ordinary course of business. (H)
- **Allocated custody and leasing do not mix.** A leased bar becomes a claim on a borrower. You can no longer say "100% allocated and unencumbered." (H)
- **Sharia.** Gold is a *ribawi* item. A fixed, guaranteed return on lent gold carries a riba risk. Compliant structures use Mudaraba or Wakala with a variable profit (AAOIFI Standard 57). (M) A "guaranteed 3%, Sharia-compliant" gold yield is a red flag.
- **VARA.** Reserve assets behind an ARVA (VARA's asset-referenced token category) must not be "rehypothecated, or subject to any pledges or encumbrances which would prevent the Issuer from processing any redemption request" (Rule III.C.3, [VARA](https://rulebooks.vara.ae/rulebook/c-reserve-assets)). (H) Leasing reserve gold is very hard to fit within that rule.
- **CBUAE.** Pooling client gold and lending it out may count as a licensed finance or deposit-taking activity. (M) In the UAE, gold lending to jewellers sits inside banks, for example Commercial Bank of Dubai's XAU Gold Metal Loan launched in June 2026. (M)
- **India.** On 8 Nov 2025 SEBI warned investors that digital gold sits outside its regulation. (H)

### 5. What this means for Aurumix
- Gold held in a vault earns nothing. Wholesale leasing pays about 0-1%. Retail leasing pays 2-4%, and that return is jeweller credit risk. (M)
- Illustration only: on US$10M leased at 3% gross, after ~1% for insurance and costs, the platform keeps about US$100k-200k a year. The downside is losing principal if a borrower defaults. (L)
- This supports the decision already in `deliverables/2-mechanism-design/_draft_allocation-and-float.md`: keep the reserve unencumbered, and consider leasing later only as a separate, opt-in product. The credit book is a cleaner source of external yield.
