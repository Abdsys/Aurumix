# How Muthoot Finance makes money from gold loans

**In one line:** Muthoot borrows at about 9%, lends against jewellery at about 20%, spends about 3% running branches, and rarely loses money because the gold is worth much more than the loan.

Confidence tags: [H] means from filings or a calculation on them, [M] means a reputable secondary source, [L] means a weak or unverified source. The RBI and Muthoot websites blocked our fetches, so the RBI rules below are [M]. Check them against the RBI text before using them in a client document.

## 1. The customer journey

1. **She walks in.** A shopkeeper needs ₹1 lakh by today. She brings her gold chain and her ID to one of Muthoot's **4,968 branches [H]** ([SMEStreet](https://smestreet.in/banking/muthoot-finance-fy26-profit-rises-98-to-10607-crore-11840960)).
2. **The gold is checked.** Staff weigh it, test the purity and take off the weight of any stones. They value it at the 22K gold price.
3. **The loan amount is set.** RBI's 2025 gold-lending rules took effect on 1 Apr 2026. They set a maximum loan-to-value (LTV) of **85% for loans up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% for ₹2.5–5 lakh and 75% above ₹5 lakh [M]**. Some loans are repaid in one go at the end ("bullet" loans). For these, the interest due at the end counts inside the limit, and the loan can run for at most **12 months [M]**. Muthoot told analysts that 85% is the legal cap, but its own target is an **LTV of about 57% [M]** ([Perivis](https://blog.perivis.com/2026/05/20/muthoot-finance-sees-record-gold-loan-aum-skips-margin-guidance/)).
4. **She gets the cash in minutes.** The gold is sealed, logged, locked in the branch strongroom and insured.
5. **The rate depends on the scheme.** Schemes run from about **12% to 24%+ a year**. Less gold cover means a higher rate **[L]**. On average Muthoot earned about **20% in FY26 [H, calculated]**.
6. **She repays or renews.** Most borrowers pay the interest and then roll the loan over or borrow more on the same gold. The loan book turns over about **every 4 months [M]** (Perivis). After full repayment, the gold must be returned within **7 working days**. If it is late, the lender pays **₹5,000 a day [M]**.
7. **If she doesn't pay,** she gets notices, and then the gold goes to public auction. The minimum auction price is **90% of market value, or 85% after two failed auctions [M]**. Anything left over after the debt is paid goes back to her.

## 2. Where Muthoot gets its money

- Muthoot is a non-bank lender (NBFC), so it cannot take savings deposits. It borrows from four places **[M; the exact mix was not in sources we could open]**:
  - bank term loans
  - bonds sold in India (NCDs)
  - overseas borrowing (ECBs and USD bonds)
  - short-term commercial paper
- **Borrowings were ₹1,38,443 cr and equity ₹37,742 cr at Mar 2026 [H]** ([Screener](https://www.screener.in/company/MUTHOOTFIN/)). That is about ₹4 borrowed for every ₹1 of its own money.
- It paid **₹9,984 cr of interest in FY26**. That is a **cost of funds of about 8.7%** on average borrowings **[H, calculated]**.

## 5. Why credit losses are tiny

- **There is far more gold than loan.** A ₹1 lakh loan sits on about ₹1.75 lakh of gold. The loan only loses money if gold falls about 40% before the lender acts.
- **The gold is easy to sell.** It can be sold anywhere within a day.
- **People come back for their jewellery.** It is family wealth, so borrowers repay to get it back.
- **Auctions recover the money.** In Q4 FY26 Muthoot recovered **₹85 cr one-off: ₹50 cr from auctions and ₹35 cr from selling bad loans to an asset reconstruction company (ARC) [M]** (Perivis).
- **The real risk is a fast gold crash on high-LTV loans.** The new 85% cap leaves a thin cushion.

**What is hard, in short:**
1. Borrowing cheaply and steadily at scale.
2. Running a trusted physical network of branches, appraisers and vaults.
3. Keeping prices up against banks.
4. Staying focused on gold loans.

An app alone solves none of these.

## 7. Gold lending in the UAE

- **A licence is required.** In the UAE, lending is limited to banks and finance companies licensed by the central bank (CBUAE). Under Decree-Law 6/2025, even *helping* someone lend without a licence is an offence. Fines start at AED 1m. Businesses had until **16 Sep 2026** to get licensed, and the ban has applied since Sep 2025 **[M]** ([Muhami](https://muhami.ae/articles/do-you-need-a-cbuae-licence-before-16-september-2026-a-simple-guide-to-the-new-uae-central-bank-law/)).
- **Banks:** one source lists gold loans at Emirates NBD (AED 10k–500k), ADCB, Mashreq (about 24-hour processing) and RAKBANK (3–36 months). It quotes **8–14% a year on a reducing balance at 70–80% LTV**, with Islamic versions structured as Murabaha **[L]** ([GoldRatesInDubai](https://goldratesindubai.com/news/gold-loan-dubai)). This is an SEO site, and Emirates NBD's product page returned a 404 when we tried it. Check each bank directly.
- **Pawn shops:** the same source mentions pawnbrokers in the Gold Souk charging **15–24% at 60–75% LTV [L]**. We found no CBUAE licence for pawnbroking. Any of this lending that is done without a lending licence is outside the law.
- **Exchange houses** (LuLu, Al Ansari) hold licences to send money and change currency, and those licences do not cover lending. LuLu has collected **Muthoot repayments from UAE-based Indians since 2022 [M]** ([APAC News](https://apacnewsnetwork.com/2022/09/muthoot-finance-ties-up-with-lulu-international-exchange-for-gold-loan-repayment/)).
- **Takeaway:** the UAE has no Muthoot-style gold lender. For banks, gold loans are a minor product. The LuLu tie-up suggests Indian workers in the UAE pledge family gold in India and repay from the UAE **[L]**.
