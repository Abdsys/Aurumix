#### What it is

Tether Gold is the largest tokenized gold product in existence: roughly US$2.46bn of market capitalisation, about 612,823 troy ounces in circulation, near enough 19 tonnes of gold behind the float. Each token represents one fine troy ounce on a specific London Good Delivery bar in a Swiss vault.

Scale is the reason it matters here, and the reason it disappoints. XAU₮ is 25 to 30 times the size of PGOLD, and its public documentation is thinner in several respects than protocols a fraction of its size. It publishes a genuinely strong quarterly reserves attestation and very little else: no whitepaper of substance, no bar list, no named custodian, no wind-down plan. Size and documentation quality are uncorrelated in this market, and XAU₮ is the proof.

The commercial model is what Aurumix should study. XAU₮ charges holders nothing to store their gold, with no annual custody fee at all. Everything else in this profile follows from that one fact.

The customer is institutional. A 50 XAU₮ direct purchase minimum (~US$200,000) and a 430 XAU₮ redemption minimum define who the issuer serves. Retail reaches XAU₮ on exchanges, in the secondary market, never through the issuer.

#### Legal structure and regulatory standing

The reserves report is unusually explicit for a crypto issuer. It states that the bars meet London Good Delivery quality assurance requirements set by the London Bullion Market Association, that the reserves are vaulted in Switzerland, and that "the Gold Reserves are owned by the XAU₮ token holders, not by the Company." Holders, it continues, "have undivided ownership rights to gold on specified gold bars," and tokens are created only after the Custodian receives the corresponding gold. That is a stronger ownership assertion than most tokenized gold products make: a direct undivided property interest in identified bars rather than a creditor claim, with the Company positioned as a non-owner. It also sits inside a document subject to reasonable assurance by BDO Italia, which gives it more weight than a marketing page.

Two caveats are material. BDO does not test the assertion as a legal conclusion; it opines that the reserves report is "fairly presented in accordance with the criteria, including Management's Key Accounting Policies, set out therein." That is an accounting conclusion about quantities and valuation, not a legal opinion that title has passed under Salvadoran or Swiss law. No trust deed, declaration of trust, bailment agreement or custody agreement is published. And "undivided ownership rights to gold on specified gold bars" is internally tense: undivided co-ownership of a pool and ownership of a specified bar are different constructs, and with 611 bars of 12.5kg backing hundreds of thousands of tokens, holders plainly cannot each own a bar. TG Commodities does not disclose which construct governs on an insolvency.

TG Commodities is registered with the CNAD as an issuer of public offerings of stablecoins, register number EME-0004, dated 19 May 2025, authorised as a Stablecoin Issuer and Digital Assets Service Provider under the LEAD, reporting to El Salvador's Financial Investigation Unit, and registered with FinCEN as a Money Services Business. The number PSAD-0032 circulates widely in secondary coverage and issuer materials. PSAD is a separate Digital Asset Service Provider register and TG Commodities does not appear in the PSAD list on the CNAD site. Both can be true at once, since the LEAD requires an issuer to have a PSAD registered for placement and marketing, but the number that verifies for issuance is EME-0004.

The jurisdictional finding is the headline. The largest tokenized gold product in the world is regulated by a stablecoin regime in El Salvador, a jurisdiction that built a bespoke digital-asset law to attract exactly this business, and it moved there from the BVI in January 2025. No securities regulator authorises it. No commodities regulator authorises it. No major-market financial regulator authorises it. A gold token is being accommodated inside a "stablecoin" category. It holds no VARA, DFSA or ADGM licence despite marketing heavily into the Gulf, reaching those markets through exchange listings and Shariah certification instead.

TG Commodities publishes no wind-down plan, no resolution procedure, no statement of how the Custodian would act on issuer failure, and no explanation of how a holder would assert the claimed ownership right against a Swiss custodian without the issuer's cooperation. For an entity this size that is a striking gap. No enforcement action against TG Commodities specific to XAU₮ was identified. Reputational risk here is inherited from the Tether group rather than generated by the gold product.

#### Custody and reserve verification

The custodian is not disclosed. Every primary document calls it only "the Custodian." Secondary sources speculate Brink's or Loomis; no primary source names it. The vault location is Switzerland, country only, with no city, facility or address. Bars are itemised by size and count and an aggregate breakdown by bar size is published, but there is no bar list: no serial numbers, no refiner names, no per-bar assay data.

The attestation deserves credit, stated precisely. It is a reasonable assurance engagement under ISAE 3000 (Revised), the higher of the two ISAE 3000 tiers and materially stronger than the limited-assurance and agreed-upon-procedures reports common in this sector. BDO states: "In our opinion, the TGRR ... is, in all material respects, fairly presented." Procedures include physical inventory to confirm the existence of fine troy ounces, reconciliation of the accounting ledger against the various blockchain ledgers relating to liabilities, and reports from a specialised provider on inventory and quality testing on a sample basis. Quarterly confirmatory assay testing of a sample of bars by a qualified independent third party is disclosed.

BDO is equally direct about the limits. "The reporting date is limited to a point in time as of 30 June 2025. We did not perform procedures or provide any assurance at any other date or time in this report." A quarterly snapshot says nothing about the other 89 days. BDO adds that "the TGRR is prepared by Management for transparency purposes. As a result, the TGRR may not be suitable for another purpose." It is not a company audit, not a financial statement audit, not a legal opinion on title, not an opinion on solvency, on custody adequacy, or on whether holder ownership rights are enforceable. Assay testing is sampled, not universal.

Smart contract audits are a separate matter and XAU₮ is much weaker there: no first-party published contract audit for XAU₮ was located. The whole position summarises as quarterly reasonable-assurance reserve attestation with sampled assay testing, no bar list, unnamed custodian, no retrievable first-party contract audit. XAU₮ is excellent at proving the gold exists and poor at explaining what a holder owns when things go wrong.

One subtlety affects any reading of the supply figures. At 30 June 2025, 246,524.33 XAU₮ had been minted, of which 186,879.51 were sold and 59,644.82 were "available for sale," held by Alpha Group Commodities, S.A. de C.V., an affiliate that also moved from the BVI to El Salvador in January 2025. At 31 March 2026, reserves stood at 707,747.139 ounces against 559,598.64 XAU₮ sold. Roughly 21% of minted supply is unsold affiliate inventory. That is why circulating supply (612,823) and total supply (707,747) diverge, and anyone reading market cap off total supply is mixing customer tokens with issuer-affiliate stock.

#### Fees, revenue and redemption

| Fee line | Rate | Paid by |
|---|---|---|
| Direct purchase / creation | 0.25%, minimum 50 XAU₮ | Buyer |
| Ongoing custody / storage | 0.00%. None | n/a |
| Management fee | None | n/a |
| Redemption to physical | 0.25% plus logistics, insurance, handling | Redeemer |
| Cash redemption from issuer | Not documented | n/a |
| Onchain transfer | Network gas only, no issuer fee | Sender |
| Cross-chain (XAUt0) | Bridge and LayerZero costs, set by the bridge | User |

Permanent free storage is the central marketing differentiator against gold ETFs, which charge 0.15% to 0.40% a year, and against PAXG. The cost is real and someone pays it.

The disclosed fee lines, 0.25% in and 0.25% out, cannot fund perpetual Swiss vaulting for a long-duration holder. Buy and hold for ten years and you pay 0.25% once while consuming ten years of allocated storage, insurance and quarterly assay testing. The arithmetic works because of what sits outside the fee table. Tether Holdings earns very large returns on its USDT reserve portfolio, against which vaulting 19 tonnes of customer gold is a rounding error, so XAU₮ is a strategic product for the group rather than a standalone profit centre. The group's own gold position has been reported at 140 to 162 tonnes, far exceeding the ~19 tonnes backing circulating XAU₮. The issuer also sells from affiliate inventory at a spread, which is revenue rather than a disclosed fee. TG Commodities has never published a statement explaining what funds free perpetual storage.

Issuance runs gold-first. The Company issues tokens after physical bars containing the corresponding fine troy ounces clear the Custodian's intake procedure. Direct issuance is KYC and AML gated with a 50 XAU₮ minimum, and pre-minted Alpha Group inventory lets direct customers be filled from stock rather than triggering a fresh mint. XAU₮ therefore has no retail primary market: the direct channel is wholesale in practice, whatever the stated policy.

Redemption is where the ownership claim and the practical reality diverge. XAU₮ tells holders they have undivided ownership rights to gold on specified bars. Exercising that right takes roughly 430 XAU₮ (~US$1.73m), a Swiss delivery address and a full KYC file. Getting the gold out of Switzerland is the holder's problem. Sanctions and jurisdictional screening apply and the excluded-country list is not published. Below one bar the ownership right is economically theoretical and the only real exit is selling the token to someone else. The claim is legally stronger than most competitors and practically inaccessible to almost every holder, simultaneously.

XAU₮ is genuinely liquid. Roughly 5.3% of market cap turns over daily across five or more venues each doing US$6m or better, and it has been reported at 54% to 60% of the entire gold-backed token market. Because it is liquid and continuously arbitrageable against spot on major venues, it trades at gold rather than above it. Deep liquidity and a durable retail premium are opposed outcomes.

Distribution runs through centralised exchange listings (Binance, OKX, Bybit, Gate, Bitfinex and many others), direct issuance for large tickets, and a growing set of DeFi and collateral integrations. Reach is global via exchanges, with explicit targeting of Indonesia (Mobee listing, July 2025) and, since Shariah certification in July 2026, the GCC, South Asia and parts of Africa.

The distribution finding is stark. The largest tokenized gold product on earth has no retail distribution mechanism of its own: no savings plan, no systematic investment plan, no recurring contribution product, no fractional accumulation programme, no referral or affiliate scheme, no agent network, no distributor commission structure. It acquires US$2.46bn through exchange listings, brand and institutional relationships alone. It is a wholesale product with a liquid secondary market, not a savings product.

Recent developments worth carrying forward. Amanah Advisors, led by Mufti Faraz Adam, issued Shariah certification on 27 July 2026 covering the ownership model, reserve transparency, physical backing and compliance with Islamic rules on gold, confirming the absence of riba, leverage and speculative derivatives. A Ledn partnership announced in June 2026 lets holders borrow against XAU₮ collateral in USDT and USAT loans, with Ledn stating a 1:1 collateral policy and no rehypothecation, and excluding the EU and Canada. Q1 2026 reserves reached 707,747.139 ounces, up 36% quarter on quarter. XAUt0 deployed on BNB Chain in March 2026 and Conflux in April 2026. Tether announced in January 2026 that XAU₮ had passed US$4bn in value, which exceeds the ~US$2.46bn circulating market cap observed in July 2026 and most likely reflects total minted supply at a peak gold price, so treat it as a marketing figure. Reuters reported in December 2025 that Tether bought roughly 27 tonnes of gold in Q4 2025, holding about 162 tonnes across the group. No depeg, incident, exploit, sustained discount or enforcement action specific to XAU₮ was identified.

#### Relevance to Aurumix

**Custody fee.** XAU₮ takes the third option: charge holders nothing and recover the cost elsewhere. No custody fee, no management fee, no grams deducted, ever. It never faces Aurumix's peg-breakage problem because it never touches the gold to pay for storage. It can do that because of a cross-subsidy Aurumix does not have. Free storage is funded by a group earning large returns on a separate stablecoin reserve book, by spread on affiliate inventory, and by a group gold position many times larger than the metal backing circulating tokens. Aurumix cannot copy the zero-fee model without a comparable external subsidy and should not assume one appears.

The strategic lesson still transfers, and it is uncomfortable. The market leader charges zero ongoing custody fee, so any Aurumix custody fee is a like-for-like competitive disadvantage that has to be justified by the savings-plan wrapper, the agent servicing and the credit facility rather than by gold storage. If Aurumix charges in cash, the messaging problem is that XAU₮ charges nothing. The answer has to be that Aurumix sells a savings programme with distribution and credit attached, not a cheaper vault.

**Dividend.** The largest, best-capitalised, most liquid tokenized gold product in the world, sitting inside a group with one of the largest profit pools in crypto, pays its holders zero. No dividend, no staking, no APY. Its Shariah certification rests explicitly on the absence of interest-based mechanisms, leverage and speculative derivatives. Tether could trivially afford a yield on 19 tonnes of gold and does not. The most plausible reading is that paying holders a return on a passive asset converts a commodity token into something resembling an investment contract, and the issuer declined that risk despite having the money. That is strong external support for caution on the ICS Dividend. Aurumix's two nearest reference points diverge completely: PGOLD promises 5% it cannot fund, XAU₮ promises nothing and leads the market. Yield is not what wins this category.

Where XAU₮ does let holders monetise without selling is credit. The Ledn partnership allows borrowing against XAU₮ collateral with no rehypothecation, which validates Aurumix's credit facility and Gold Card as the better-designed value-add: holder utility, issuer revenue, no encumbrance on the gold, no securities-classification problem. Ledn's exclusion of the EU and Canada is a useful precedent for jurisdictional gating of a credit product.

**Token standard.** XAU₮ is a plain ERC-20 and that choice cost it nothing, because it has nothing to protect. No tier standing, no dividend entitlement, no credit eligibility, no buyback right. It is a bearer claim on an ounce of gold, so maximum transferability with no compliance logic at the token layer is exactly right for it, and US$130m of daily volume is the direct product of that choice.

This confirms Aurumix's ERC-3643 lean and also prices it. XAU₮ shows what permissionless buys: deep liquidity, universal listings, ~5.3% daily turnover. Aurumix's ICS standing, dividend, credit eligibility and buyback rights all break on an anonymous transfer, so it cannot have that. Make the trade with eyes open. Choosing ERC-3643 means accepting that Aurumix will never have XAU₮-like secondary liquidity, and therefore that the cash buyback is the only real exit. That is coherent, but the buyback must then be resourced properly, because there is no deep market to fall back on.

The XAUt0 experience is a subtler warning. Tether extended to seven or more chains through a LayerZero OFT wrapper. XAU₮ on Ethereum is the canonical token carrying the stated ownership right in Swiss gold. XAUt0 elsewhere is, under the standard OFT design, a bridged representation backed by canonical XAU₮ locked in a lockbox contract. Whether an XAUt0 holder on Solana or TON has the same legal claim on physical gold is not disclosed. No redemption path exists for XAUt0 without bridging back first. Who controls the lockbox, and what happens to XAUt0 holders if the bridge fails or is exploited, is not disclosed either. An XAUt0 holder carries gold price risk, issuer risk, custodian risk and bridge risk, the last entirely undocumented. If Aurumix ever wraps a permissioned base token into a transferable ERC-20 for liquidity it creates this exact problem: a wrapper holder with the economic exposure and none of the ICS standing, dividend entitlement or credit eligibility. Decide in advance what a wrapped AURX holder owns, and write it down. XAU₮ has not, at US$2.5bn.

**Regulatory route.** A large, sophisticated issuer chose a bespoke small-jurisdiction regime over a major-market licence. TG Commodities moved from the BVI to El Salvador and registered under the LEAD as CNAD issuer EME-0004. It is licensed by a purpose-built digital-asset regulator in a small jurisdiction, not by a securities or commodities regulator, and not by VARA, DFSA or ADGM despite marketing into the GCC. That informs Aurumix's cost/benefit on VARA: the market leader concluded that a light-touch bespoke regime plus global exchange distribution beat an expensive major-market licence.

The analogy has a hard limit. XAU₮ is a pure custody receipt with no yield, no credit and no retail savings plan, so it fits a stablecoin-issuer box cleanly. Aurumix's hybrid of dividend, credit, SIP and agent network fits no single box anywhere, which is why its regulatory problem is harder than XAU₮'s at a fraction of the size. XAU₮'s route is available to Aurumix only if Aurumix strips back to the gold core.

Treat the Shariah certification as a distribution instrument rather than a licence. Tether reached the GCC and South Asia through certification plus exchange listings, with no local licensing at all. For a product targeting India, the UAE and NRIs that is a cheap, fast credential with disproportionate reach, and Amanah Advisors and Mufti Faraz Adam are a named, checkable precedent. Aurumix's gold core should certify readily. The ICS Dividend and the 90% to 95% LTV credit facility are what will attract scrutiny, since riba and leverage are exactly what the XAU₮ certification confirmed were absent. A second, independent reason for caution on the dividend.

**Redemption.** XAU₮ has physical redemption on paper and effectively none in practice: ~430 XAU₮ (~US$1.73m), Swiss-only delivery, full KYC, 0.25% plus logistics. Aurumix should take real comfort here, because the market leader's redemption right is inaccessible to essentially every retail holder. The gap between "you own physical gold" and "you can get it" is the industry norm, not an Aurumix defect. The honest differentiator is disclosure: XAU₮ asserts strong ownership language while gating redemption at US$1.73m, and Aurumix saying plainly "no physical redemption, cash buyback only" is more transparent than that. Say so, rather than hiding it.

**Premium and liquidity.** XAU₮ is the strongest evidence against a 3–8% premium thesis. ~US$2.46bn AUM, ~US$130m daily volume, ~5.3% turnover across five or more venues, continuous arbitrage against spot, and it trades at gold. Set alongside PGOLD, which has no liquidity and no reliable premium, the rule is that liquidity and premium are inversely related, and a durable 3–8% premium cannot survive a liquid market. If Aurumix's premium thesis depends on AURX trading above net asset value, that premium can only exist in a market too thin or too closed to arbitrage, which sits badly with any liquidity ambition and is hard to defend to a regulator. Stress-test the revenue model with the premium set to zero. The permissioned ERC-3643 route, by limiting free transfer, is arguably the only structure in which a controlled premium is sustainable at all, and if so it should be stated as a deliberate design choice rather than a side effect.

**Proof of reserve.** XAU₮ sets an achievable benchmark: quarterly ISAE 3000 (Revised) reasonable assurance by BDO Italia, with physical inventory procedures, sampled third-party assay testing and reconciliation of the ledger against onchain liabilities, plus a daily transparency page. That is better than the tokenized-gold norm and is what a serious UAE retail product should match. The remaining gaps show where cheap differentiation sits: no bar list, no named custodian, sampled rather than full assay, point-in-time quarterly snapshots. Naming a custodian and publishing a bar list would put Aurumix ahead of a US$2.5bn incumbent on transparency at near-zero cost. Publish reserve attestation and smart-contract audit separately and label them clearly, because XAU₮ is strong on one and weak on the other and the two are routinely conflated.

**Distribution.** This is where Aurumix's moat sits, and XAU₮ proves it. No savings plan, no recurring purchase, no referral programme, no agent network, and a US$200,000 direct minimum. The largest tokenized gold product in the world does not compete with Aurumix for the retail monthly saver at all; it is a liquidity venue and a benchmark, not a distribution competitor. XAU₮ owns institutional and treasury demand and will keep owning it. The US$20-a-month Indian or NRI saver is a market it has made no attempt to serve. Aurumix's SIP and 3-tier agent network are a different business, not a weaker version of XAU₮'s. The posture should be to out-distribute XAU₮ rather than out-vault it, and to be honest internally that Aurumix's product risk is distribution and compliance risk (agent conduct, commissions, mis-selling, KYC at scale) rather than gold-custody risk. Note too that the Shariah certification targets the GCC, South Asia and parts of Africa, so expect XAU₮ brand presence in Aurumix's exact geographies without direct product competition.

**Wind-down.** XAU₮ has no published wind-down plan at US$2.5bn: no resolution procedure, no statement of what happens to the gold, no explanation of how holders would assert their claimed ownership rights against an unnamed Swiss custodian if TG Commodities failed. A published wind-down plan would differentiate Aurumix against the category leader, not merely against failed micro-caps. It is a gap nobody in the category has filled.

Takeaways for Aurumix:

- Do not build a business case that assumes a custody fee is normal. The market leader charges zero, so any Aurumix fee must be justified on the savings and credit wrapper rather than the vault.
- Treat XAU₮'s zero yield as the strongest available evidence that a dividend is not required to accumulate gold-token AUM, and that adding one carries regulatory and Shariah risk.
- Copy the Ledn structure rather than a dividend: credit against holdings, no rehypothecation, jurisdictionally gated.
- Match quarterly ISAE 3000 reasonable assurance at minimum, then beat XAU₮ cheaply by naming the custodian and publishing a bar list.
- Decide now what a wrapped AURX holder owns, before any liquidity wrapper is built.
- Price Shariah certification into the plan as a distribution credential, and expect the dividend and high-LTV credit to draw the scrutiny.
