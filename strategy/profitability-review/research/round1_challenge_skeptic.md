### Unsupported or internally wrong (not checked online; found by reading the memos against the dossier)

- **Distribution memo: kilobars save "2–2.5pp, about USD 15 a customer".** This is out of date. Decision D28 already halved the premium to 1.5%. The internal premium line is USD 4.99 per payer, so the realistic saving is USD 3–4. The credit memo gets this right.
- **Distribution memo: per-user price of USD 0.50–1.00 a month and serving cost of USD 3.** Market evidence (about 0.85% gross at SafeGold) puts a USD 40 saver at about USD 4 a year. The model's own serving cost is USD 10.73.
- **Consumer memo: the jeweller pays a 7.5% maturity bonus *and* a 3–4% referral.** That is 10–11% of the set. Dubai making charges are AED 15–60/g, or 3–12%, so no jeweller will fund both.
- **Consumer memo: a USD 6/month family tier.** That is 8–30% of a USD 20–75 contribution, asked of a customer base with an "LIC policyholder mindset". There is no evidence for it.
- **Credit memo: USD 2,000 balance, USD 900 drawn, 15–18% APR.** The model's credit limit is USD 205. The memo prices above bank gold loans (8–14%) for a smaller, riskier borrower.
- **Credit memo: "80k payers, inside the plan's 88.6k".** The 88.6k was reached with the paid acquisition the memo cuts. The argument is circular.
- **Consumer memo: "every gift recipient is a free new account".** Only UAE residents who pass KYC can receive gifts. Recipients in India are blocked, because the India route is closed.
- **All three: India jewellery bars pay about 6% duty, and onboarding at an exchange house takes 6–12 months.** Both are plausible but unsourced.

## 2. Attacking the moves

**Moves that depend on a regulated activity Aurumix does not hold:**

- **Credit memo, Move 1.** Servicing loans, running the LTV engine, acting as collateral agent, and a 45–50% risk share backed by a USD 0.7m first-loss deposit. This is credit activity under CBUAE. The deposit would come out of a company with a USD 4.8m p90 funding hole. The only legal role is a referral and technology provider working under the lender's licence, and even that needs an Article 62 analysis.
- **Credit memo, Move 2.** A "payday sweep inside Aurumix's own ledger" means holding customer money. That needs a stored-value or issuer licence. The sweep has to sit on the issuer's ledger.
- **Credit memo Move 5 and distribution memo Move 3: collecting Muthoot repayments.** Only an exchange house can do this. LuLu can because it holds that licence.
- **Leasing customer gold.** Allocated holdings become credit exposure, and that conflicts with the trust structure.
- **A discretionary AI manager.** It needs a VARA Management & Investment Services licence, a CMA licence or a DFSA licence.

**Fantasies for a pre-licence company that needs about USD 5m:**

- Risk-share lending.
- USD 600k a year from a single co-brand partner with 50k actives. OGold has 1.5m users across its whole app.
- A family subscription carrying a USD 18-per-account uplift.
- Selling CG's API at USD 50–100k setup before any partner has signed.

**Moves that are real:**

- Fixing the cost stack.
- Redeem-to-jewellery as a referral.
- One distribution partner priced at market rates.
- A card positioned for the trip home, at net economics.
- The distribution memo's kill criterion: no signed LOI by the end of Q1 2027 means no further VARA spend.

## 4. What all three memos missed

1. **The token is the cause of the margin problem.** VARA ARVA status costs AED 300k a year in fees, AED 1.5m of capital and attestation costs, and it is the reason buyback must be fee-free. Digital-gold businesses earn on *both* sides of the spread. Aurumix gave up the sell side by choosing to be an ARVA. SafeGold shows a non-token route already operating in the UAE: a DMCC entity working with a CBUAE-licensed wallet partner, charging a service fee and storage. Freezing or deferring the token reopens a 0.5–1.5% buyback spread, cuts the funding need, and changes no feature customers can see. Counsel must confirm the CMA and CBUAE Article 62 position.
2. **Distribute instead of operating.** All three memos keep Aurumix running the vault, treasury and attestations. Running the product on a SafeGold, OGold or DMCC bullion-house backend removes the 8.7-day treasury cycle, the vault cost and the minting question. Scarce cash then goes into the NRI channel, which is the asset that actually earns.
3. **Pricing presentation.** A visible 5% fee sits next to ENBD's and Liv's "AED 0 fee". Every competitor in the market prices through the spread.
4. **The regulatory map has changed.** The memos predate the switch from SCA to CMA and the CBUAE facilitation rule. Every referral, card and credit idea has to be structured under a licensed partner.
5. **Gold is down 27% from January.** Savers with an "LIC mindset" are sitting on losses. Retention messaging needs to cover cost averaging and Sharia certification (OGold has Al Huda) before any upsell.
6. **The fallback.** No memo names an exit or merger. Joyalukkas (a jewellery group that also owns an exchange house) or Al Ansari are logical acquirers of a working NRI gold-savings book.
