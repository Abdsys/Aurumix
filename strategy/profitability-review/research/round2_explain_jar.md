# How Jar makes money, and whether half of it really comes from jewellery

## Short answer
- **The "half from jewellery" claim is mostly wrong.** It divides a full-price jewellery sales figure (₹100 cr+) by a net company revenue figure (₹208 cr). The two are not like for like.
- Jewellery earns Jar the most on each sale. But it is probably **only about 5–15% of what Jar actually keeps**, not half (Low, my estimate below).
- Most of Jar's money still comes from the **spread on daily gold saving**: the gap between the price it sells gold at and the price it pays for it.

## Why the "half" claim breaks
1. **There are two kinds of revenue here.** In FY24 Jar was a middleman and booked only its commission. In FY25 it became the seller and books the full price of the gold ([Behind the Feature](https://behindthefeature.substack.com/p/how-jar-built-a-2450-crore-business)). The ₹208 cr is Jar's own cleaned-up figure.
2. **Jewellery is counted at full price.** A ₹50,000 necklace counts as ₹50,000 of revenue, even though roughly ₹40,000–45,000 of that is gold passed on to the maker.
3. **So ₹100 cr ÷ ₹208 cr mixes a full-price number with a net number.** That inflates jewellery's share.
4. **Not every Nek sale is saved gold being converted.** Nek also sells silver, diamond and lab-grown diamond pieces, and anyone can pay in cash.
5. **The timing is short.** Nek launched around October 2024 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/wealthtech-startup-jar-halves-fy25-loss-to-inr-50-5-cr/)), so "₹100 cr a year" is probably an annualised run-rate (Low).

**Rough corrected split (Low, my estimate):**
- Jewellery: ₹100 cr of sales × the 8–15% Jar keeps = **₹8–15 cr**.
- Everything Jar keeps across the business: **₹114–208 cr**.
- So jewellery is about **5–15%** of what Jar keeps. The spread on daily saving is most of the rest.

## How a saver turns gold into jewellery, step by step
Jar does not publish its exact terms. This is the usual flow, based on Nek and on SafeGold's jeweller partners (Low for Jar-specific details).
1. **Save.** The customer saves ₹10 a day through UPI autopay. The app buys gold at its selling price, which carries a 2–5% spread plus 3% GST ([OroPocket](https://blog.oropocket.com/digital-gold-charges-in-india-explained-spreads-gst-storage-and-selling-fees/), Med). The gold sits in a vault; Brinks is Jar's custodian (Med).
2. **Choose.** The customer picks a piece on Nek.
3. **Price.** Gold weight × today's rate, plus a making charge, plus 3% GST.
4. **Pay.** The saved grams cover the gold part. The customer pays the making charge, the GST and any shortfall by UPI.
5. **Make and ship.** A partner manufacturer makes or ships the piece. Jar holds no stock (Med).
6. **Split.** The maker gets the gold value and most of the making charge. Jar keeps a markup.

**Example (illustrative):** a customer has saved ₹20,000 of gold.
- On the way in, Jar earned about ₹400–1,000 from the 2–5% spread.
- The customer then buys earrings: ₹20,000 of gold plus a ₹3,000 making charge (15%) plus GST.
- Jar might keep ₹1,500–3,000 on that one sale.

So one conversion earns about 2–5x the saving margin. That makes each converting customer valuable, but only a minority convert.

## Where the jewellery margin really sits
- **Making charges in India:** Tanishq chains 9–25% and bangles 16–25%. Malabar chains 13–19% ([Gullak, Oct 2026](https://gullak.money/blog/jewellery-making-charges/), Med).
- **Titan (Tanishq's parent):** jewellery operating margin of 11.2% in FY26. Higher coin sales pulled margins down ([ICICI Direct Q4FY26](https://www.icicidirect.com/mailcontent/idirect_titan_q4fy26.pdf), High).
- **Dubai making charges:** 3–8% for plain pieces, 8–15% standard, 15–25% handmade ([GoldRatesInDubai](https://goldratesindubai.com/dubai-gold-making-charges), Med).
- **What this means for an app:** an app that only drop-ships gets a slice of the making charge. The maker and the store keep most of it.

## The plain lesson: where the money is
- **The gold itself pays almost nothing.** SafeGold moves ₹6,867 cr of gold and keeps 0.03%.
- **People pay for three things around the gold:**
  - **Convenience:** the 2–5% saving spread. It goes to whoever owns the customer's daily habit.
  - **Craft:** the 8–25% making charge. It goes mostly to the jeweller who makes and sells the piece.
  - **Time:** interest-free scheme cash and gold loans.
- **Jar's profit comes mostly from convenience.** Jewellery is a high-margin but small top-up.
- **For a new entrant:** owning the saving habit is the base business. The full making charge goes to whoever owns the jewellery store. An app that only refers customers gets a slice.
